1Start here

In the year 102,187 After Founding, a single state keeps the peace of seven galaxies. Its citizens call it the Keeping. In the High Speech it is Yūghum, the Yoke. It shelters 85.633 quadrillion lives of 513 sentient peoples across 774 million worlds and habitats, and it has not known hunger or poverty for fifty thousand years.

At its summit stand 56,250 Tenēren: tall, luminous, long-lived descendants of a stranded human colony that kept faith with the ancient alien beasts of its twilight cradle world, and learned from them how to bend the hidden folds of space with bare flesh. They dwell in high metallic splendour as constitutional autocrats bound by a written trust that the galaxies can revoke—and every one of them is bound by law to grow old, hand back the ring of office, and die in their own natural time.

This codex opens at the still noon of thirty-six thousand years without an equal in war—in the very season when an unreckoned music first drifts across the dark beyond the seventh sky.

The scale in five numbers

  • 7 galaxies. One vast barred super-spiral 275,000 light-years across and its six satellite galaxies: 4.4 trillion stars in all.
  • 289 million settled star systems. Roughly one star in every fifteen thousand. The remaining 99.993% belongs to the wild Dark—within the Keeping’s writ, and left untouched by constitutional law.
  • 85.633 quadrillion citizens. Baseline humans make up roughly 2.5%—one of the two or three largest single peoples, leaving the Keeping 97.5% non-human.
  • 20 billion vessels and 500 trillion personnel under arms, projecting deterrence across all seven galaxies, holding outer red lines against lawless pockets, and answering planetary disasters before the next dawn.
  • 56,250 Tenēren. One for every 1.5 trillion citizens.

Three ideas that carry everything else

  1. Power is a marriage of threads and hidden shape. Space is an eleven-dimensional cloth. Its tension is carried by an elusive particle, the xiōn, which can be gathered, stored, spent and fought over; its hidden geometry holds seven tightly curled directions, the Fold (Kṣōng), which can be sloped, pleated, stiffened or cut. Neither half works alone: threads without shape are only light, and shape refuses to bend without loose threads granting it room to yield. Every wonder in this sky—from winged battle stations to hovering crowns—pays twin prices: waste heat, and a three-colour glow of purple, gold and crimson.
  2. The rulers are revered constitutional autocrats, and they stay mortal. The Twenty Houses live in radiant splendour as emblems of the realm’s dignity, yet hold no private monopolies or inherited fortunes. They cannot multiply beyond the carrying breath of twenty wild groves, earn high office strictly by blind merit among their peers, and age naturally to death around two centuries.
  3. The peace is real, and its tensions are honest. There is no cartoonish rot or secret court poison hidden beneath the gilded stone. The Keeping’s frictions grow straight out of its own virtues: unimaginable scale, thirty-six millennia without defeat, fifty millennia of deliberately bounded machine minds, the grief of unequal lifespans, and the pride and poise of a civilization that has proved itself right for a hundred thousand years.

How to read this codex

The chronicle moves from the skies and wonders of the realm inward to its people, its laws, its wars and the family standing at its centre; the deep craft of its language, master ledger and glossary wait at the close. Alongside the main text sit three companions: brief In daily life glimpses of how an ordinary citizen walks beneath all this majesty; folded Archivist’s notes tucked neatly into the margins for readers who wish to inspect the mechanics, astronomy and statecraft behind the veil; and crimson-edged Author’s design notes marking the structural tensions left open on purpose.

A brief key for sounding the High Speech (Ṣāngvāk)—the full grammar and history wait in section 16:

  • A line over a vowel makes it long: ā as in “father”, ē as in “vein”, ī as in “machine”, ō as in “throne”, ū as in “moon”, and ü / ǖ as in Mandarin ü or French u.
  • An h after a stop or affricate marks aspiration only—a clean breath on both voiceless (ph, th, ṭh, ch, kh) and voiced consonants (bh, dh, ḍh, jh, gh), never English “f” or “th”.
  • A dot beneath a letter curls the tongue back (retroflex): ṭ, ṭh, ḍ, ḍh, ṇ, ṛ, ḷ, ṣ. Plain l is always light and clear.
  • Flat alveolo-palatals borrow clean Latin letters: c is an unaspirated flat “j/ch” (Mandarin j), ch its puffed partner (Mandarin q), j / jh their voiced pair, and x a soft flat “sh” (Mandarin x, Sanskrit ś). ṃ nasalises the vowel before it.
  • Four tones shape meaning on short and long vowels alike. In running prose, tone accents appear chiefly on the low, falling breath of the hollow tone (◌̂, naming absence, frontiers and endings), the high, crisp striking tone (◌́, naming motion and acts), and the dipping leaning tone (◌̌, naming poised potential).

2The Seven-Ring Crown

Seven galaxies hang bound together in one deep well of dark matter and starlight. In the High Speech they are called Chīcakkōr, “the Seven-Wheel Crown.” The earliest star-chart makers drew the six satellite galaxies as pale rings turning about the vast parent spiral, and the image endured across five hundred tongues.

The Great Wheel

Gravōrum, the Great Wheel, is a barred super-spiral 275,000 light-years from rim to rim—nearly three times the span of Old Earth’s Milky Way—lit by roughly 3.9 trillion stars.

  • The Core burns dense, ancient and fierce, serving as the great natural spring of the Keeping’s physics. Its central engine, pulsars and dead suns shake loose an endless outward gale of free xiōns—the thread-wind (section 3). High above that blazing heart, sheltered in a quiet pocket of the Core halo, turns Suttīḷis, the untouched cradle world of the Tenēren, kept under silent orbital watch for ninety-three thousand years.
  • The Central Bar is the forge-heart of the Crown: luminous shipyards, lantern-world clusters, and the busiest Gate traffic in known space.
  • The Arms and Spurs shelter most living worlds. The capital, Jhilvāng, rests on the Quiet Spur—a tranquil, metal-rich mid-disc reach chosen because no star within a thousand light-years is forecast to die violently for ten million years.

The Six Lamps

Six companion galaxies—from starburst inner dwarfs to wide calm halo spirals—turn about the Great Wheel, holding roughly 500 billion stars among them. From a dark hillside on a clear night they hang above the local constellations like six faint smudges of coloured smoke, which ordinary speech calls the Lamps.

The archipelago & the wild Dark

Even after a hundred thousand years, the Keeping does not cover the sky like a blanket; it lights it like an archipelago. Of 4.4 trillion stars, 289 million are settled—roughly one sun in every fifteen thousand—gathered into 18.4 million Clusters anchored on quiet eddies of the thread-wind. On sparse outer rims and in Dvā̂ṇis, a Cluster may hold only two to six suns; along the blazing Core-rim and Central Bar, eighty to a hundred and twenty. Yet every Cluster shares one strict measure: no inhabited world lies farther than 12.3 light-years from its Gate hub—a single day’s full Run for a warship or relief ark.

Between these islands of lamp-lit sky stretch 150 to 400 light-years of untouched wilderness—the Dark. Under Tabūḷ VIII, no star may be claimed without proven demographic need, and peacetime fleets are barred from sweeping wild systems unprovoked. Yet the Keeping’s writ reaches every corner of all seven galaxies: no rival peer power is acknowledged anywhere inside the Crown.

Archivist’s note · On Cluster spacing, wild volume, and fleet reach

Across the seven galaxies, 18.4 million Clusters of radius 12.3 light-years occupy roughly 0.064% of galactic volume—averaging 16 settled star systems, 4 major worlds, and 38 lesser moons, asteroids and habitats per Cluster (42 inhabited bodies in all), with a mean separation of ~200 light-years across the intervening Dark.

Each Cluster hub stations roughly 1,100 fleet vessels (~430 warships alongside relief arks, transports and auxiliaries). At a warship’s full Run of 4,500 times light-speed, even the midpoint of the widest 400-light-year Dark gap is crossed in under eighteen days, and most wild stars lie within ten.

Free holds & the Four Outer Writ Red Lines

The Dark is wild, not empty. Tucked inside dusty nebulae, red-dwarf pockets and unmapped belts across all seven galaxies sit scattered free holds—independent microstates of one to a few dozen stars flying their own banners and keeping their own petty republics, merchant guilds or crowns. None approaches even a twentieth of the Keeping’s local weight.

They govern themselves outside the Keeping’s domestic law, yet live permanently within its long shadow—conducting their ambitions quietly and anxiously beneath four absolute Red Lines enforced without warning by Sēnā Dvīmaṇḍōr:

  1. No slavery or legal ownership of any sentient being.
  2. No unshackled or self-improving AI (Tabūḷ IX).
  3. No unauthorised Gates or Needles capable of scarring space or bypassing hub controls.
  4. No predatory war-fleets menacing neighbouring stars or Cluster lanes.

Over the centuries many free holds petition to enter the Keeping once their citizens look across the border at the Hearth; those that prize their own banners stay courteous, wary, and careful never to cross a line drawn without their consent.

Archivist’s note · On the Outer Writ and sovereign free holds

Candid jurists in Sabhā Sarvavāk admit what imperial hymns soften: enforcing the Four Red Lines upon unrepresented microstates is benevolence backed by overwhelming force. House Sāṃhuvāḷan defends it as regional self-defence—an unshackled ASI or a torn spatial seam honours no border—yet frontier republics frequently regard the Outer Writ as an unspoken empire peering over their garden wall.

Author’s design note · Why the Dark stays wildLeaving 99.993% of stars unsettled under Tabūḷ VIII—while tying 6 billion patrol cruisers to settled-system security (~21 cutters per settled star) and barring random naval sweeps of wild space—is a deliberate architectural choice. It preserves frontier mystery, nervous free holds, smugglers, waking Iron Apostasy derelicts, and the silent ruins of Ghuḍḍān inside a 100,000-year-old multigalactic superpower without shrinking the Keeping’s real reach.

Where Old Earth lies

Nobody knows. The Milky Way appears on no star-chart in Chīcakkōr. The Keeping’s oldest memory—kept closest by the Tenēren—says that humankind came as colonists sent out from Old Earth, though how their arks crossed the intergalactic night, or why they fled, no archive can tell. Only two mute witnesses endure: the Old Tear beside Suttīḷis, a pre-Keeping wound in space where the lead ark came down and whose rim still bleeds free threads into the halo, and the cold, unlabelled stonework buried deep in the crust of Ghuḍḍān. Neither speaks.

In daily life. Your Cluster is your home country—two suns on a quiet frontier, dozens in the glittering Bar, all within a day of one another. The Dark beyond is deep sea, and the Gates are the grand ferries. Most citizens live full lives close to home; yet almost every child has stood on a cool stone terrace after dusk and learned to pick out emerald Vīḷüṇ and the faint, lonely smear of Dvā̂ṇis hanging above the horizon.

3The threads and the Fold

Everything in the Keeping that looks to a stranger like high sorcery springs from a single unified craft: weavecraft, a marriage of quantum particle mastery and higher-dimensional geometry. Threadwork gathers, stores and directs an elusive subatomic particle, the xiōn. Foldwork spends those threads to slope, pleat, stiffen or cut the hidden dimensions of space. Neither half stands alone: threads without geometry are only glow, and geometry refuses to bend until loose threads give the cloth slack enough to yield.

The eleven-dimensional cloth

Spacetime is an eleven-dimensional cloth: three open directions of ordinary space, one of time, and seven spatial directions curled impossibly tight beneath the scale of atoms. Think of a bridge cable seen from a distant hill: to the eye it is a single line, yet an ant walking along it can also walk around it. Every point in the sky holds seven unseen “arounds.”

The xiōn is the tension-quantum of the Fold, as the photon is the quantum of light. It pays no heed to electric charge or nuclear force, drifting straight through ordinary stone and flesh unnoticed—which is why no other local civilization ever built a dial that could feel it. Wind, slack & thinstone

Ordinary reactors cannot make loose threads; only extreme gravitational and magnetic violence—galactic cores, magnetars, neutron stars, dying supergiants, and wounds like the Old Tear—can shake them free. They stream outward across the galaxies as thread-wind, knitting back rapidly inside deep solar gravity wells (making space near suns stiff and taut) while pooling for centuries in quiet eddies and green nebular dust (slack). Two accidents of birth handed the Tenēren’s ancestors this hidden layer of nature: descending beside the Old Tear beneath a neutron star, and walking on canyons veined with ḍhāṇsiḷ (thinstone), a blue-black glassy mineral whose crystal lattice traps loose xiōns as lodestone grabs iron. Today synthetic lattice is grown by the megaton.

Every great machine in the Keeping runs on two currencies that never substitute for each other. Fusion and antimatter pay the energy—the raw work required to climb a gravity well, drive a hull or fire a lance. Pooled charge (xiōns) pays the compliance—the consumable working fluid that lets energy reach into the seven curled dimensions before settling home in crimson light.

The four workings

1. Lift (lēvār). Weaving pooled threads into the Fold around an object lightens both its gravitational weight and its inertia together, though every atom stays present and true mass never touches zero. A harbour hand can glide a forty-tonne freight bed sideways with one palm, yet Lift never cheats the ledger of climb: hauling stone up a spire still pays its full height in power. When Lift shuts off mid-motion, the whole load and every unstrapped crate inside its field brake smoothly together in a brief crimson flash.

Archivist’s note · On Lift, inertia, and why falling freight does not smash walls

Lift couples mass symmetrically into the Fold relative to the local Weave rest frame, lowering effective inertial and gravitational coupling together (M → meff). Vertical ascent still draws full gravitational work (mgh) from onboard cells, while horizontal push pays only Δ½meffv2—and because Lift engages only against local rest, it cannot be exploited to build a perpetual-motion catapult.

Should Lift cut out mid-glide, momentum conservation slows the entire enclosed mass proportionally at once (v′ = (meff/M)v), shedding the small residual difference as crimson light rather than hurling loose cargo across a deck.

2. Run (dhrā́v). Packing threads tightly into the Fold ahead of a hull while letting them slacken behind slopes spacetime itself, sliding a calm pocket faster than light without locally breaking light’s speed. Anchored motionless inside starship deck-plating (bhāṛpaṭ) or the inner shell of a lantern-world (kōṣ), the same working casts a steady 1g floor of artificial gravity—unifying star-flight and artificial worlds under one law.

3. Pleat (diē̌l). Two crystal lattices grown as one stone and parted share a single pool of threads across any gulf. Driving that pool pleats the Fold between them until both ring-planes touch as one threshold (a Gate). Reversing the pleat stretches the approach path outward instead, curving blows and fire harmlessly past a warded ship or body.

4. Cut (chē̂t). Emptying a single microscopic point of every free and bound thread causes its seven curled dimensions to unfurl locally. Gravity’s threshold collapses so sharply that focused gamma-beams fold pure light into a spinning micro-black hole, shearing the cloth of space open onto the Deep (Xuānum).

Listening is the quiet fifth sense: a lattice feels the drift of thread-wind and the slightest tremor across a shared crystal pool instantly.

Threadwork without folding, two accounts & hard limits

Even without bending the Fold, pure threadwork underpins daily craft: glowing violet stores (xiōkūp), kilometre-wide wind-nets (fēngjāl) billowing beside magnetars, stiffening (locking bulkheads or armour rigid against shock), draining (stripping charge to collapse wards or halt runaway rigs), and thread-ink (marks of sworn office). Every working still pays two accounts: spent charge and immediate waste heat. That is why nothing grand in the Keeping hides its heat; every spire, wing and crown is a radiator turned into art. Five hard bounds govern all of it: weavecraft needs slack, makes no free energy, never breaks causality, scars space for millennia if a Cut closes clumsily, and grows steeply hungrier in charge and heat with cross-sectional size and surface area.

Archivist’s note · Why civilian cities do not drain the Thread Harvest

Engineers of House Vāstūren divide weavecraft into two distinct regimes: static loops and open burns. Everyday civic wonders—lifted towers, lūcīng drop-shafts, freight beds, and bhāṛpaṭ gravity decks—trap pooled charge inside closed, low-loss crystal circuits much like a superconducting magnet. Laying down a new spire or lantern-world demands an immense upfront charge deposit, yet once seated, its lattice holds the Fold with only a negligible trickle of bleed easily replenished from the local Cluster’s natural slack eddy.

Dynamic workings are another matter altogether. Superluminal Runs, Gate Tides, heavy combat Wards, Drain-lances, and Needle Cuts force vast volumes of running threads through open geometry, settling them straight back into bound crimson light. That relentless open-loop burn—multiplied across twenty billion warships and eighteen million pulsing Gate hubs—is what keeps magnetar wind-nets and the emerald harvest fleets of Vīḷüṇ indispensable.

Archivist’s note · On causality and the cosmic rest compass

Every shared-pool pulse, Gate crossing and Fold shortcut measures simultaneity along the Weave’s preferred cosmic microwave rest frame, rendering closed timelike loops physically impossible. One useful consequence prized by House Nebhālor navigators is that any calibrated thinstone array doubles as an absolute velocity compass against the cosmic background.

In daily life. Citizens walk inside weavecraft as Old Earth walked inside electricity. In spire-cities you step off a nine-hundred-storey balcony into a glowing lūcīng (light-well) and drift downward like a slow leaf. Winter flagstones stay warm beneath bare soles because basement xiōkūp wells breathe their Lift heat out through ornamental cooling-groves (frīlīn) of stepped bronze and bismuth trees. By constitutional law every inhabited tower must stand for thirty days with Lift completely dead; once a year each city drains its ribs for a single quiet hour, so bridges groan, purple veins go dark, and citizens remember what their spires truly weigh.

4The look of things

When a civilization holds limitless metal, a hundred unhurried millennia of craft, and a physics that makes every working shine, nothing built to last is left plain. Every silhouette in the Keeping is splendour that works: sweeping wings are radiators, halos are settling threadlight, violet veins are charge conduits, impossible height is Lift worn proudly in the open air. Its architecture braids the vertical soar of Gothic cathedrals with the layered, sculptural richness of classical South Asian temples and stepwells—dressed in the full spectrum of coloured metals without a drop of paint: interference-anodised peacock titanium and niobium, rainbow stepped bismuth, rose and green and violet golds, deep cobalt steel, verdigris copper, and dark glassy thinstone.

Red, gold and purple: the Three Threads

The physical triad of threadlight—purple at rest, gold at work, crimson in settling—echoed the oldest ceremonial hues carried aboard the human arks: Roman imperial purple, Chinese imperial gold, and Punjabi bridal crimson. Sabhā Chīcakkōr wears all three together. Every civic law-pillar is gold alloy inlaid with violet glass and edged in red; mourning wears crimson alone, the colour of a spent thread returning home.

Jhilvāng, the Capital

Home to two trillion citizens on the Quiet Spur, Jhilvāng is a world-city girdled by two immense orbital rings—the inner of violet niobium, the outer of rose gold—rooted to the planetary crust by sixty-four colossal lift-stems. Rising outward from both rings like a child’s drawing of the sun reach tens of thousands of slender needle-spires up to 1,200 kilometres long: docks, high courts, academies and palaces that serve above all as luminous radiators venting the city’s vast warmth into space. By night each spire glows dull cherry-crimson along its spine, violet along its charge ribs, and bright gold with streaming orbital traffic.

Though the capital’s bones still honour its founding metallic silhouettes, ninety millennia as the seat of five hundred peoples have braided countless off-world aesthetics into its skyline. High above the gilded stepwells rise water-curtained concourses, acoustic galleries shaped for echolocation, living mineral canopies, and stark volcanic-glass bridges drawn from worlds whose suns look nothing like the Quiet Spur—fusions so deeply woven into court life that Tenēren fashion itself borrows as eagerly from outer-rim ateliers as from ancestral looms. Kilometres beneath those sunlit terraces lie sealed, lantern-lit strata from vanished eras: the drowned basalt colonnades of the Third Long Millennium and the gilded aqueduct-halls of forgotten ages, stone witnesses that even a golden plateau rests upon deep time.

Archivist’s note · The heat ledger of a two-trillion-soul capital

Two trillion post-scarcity citizens generate roughly twelve times a planet’s normal solar insolation (~2 × 1018 watts). The sixty-four tens-of-kilometres-wide lift-stems carry Fold-assisted thermal shunts and plasma-loop conduits straight up into the rings and their ~30,000 radiating spires, whose tens of millions of square kilometres of dull-crimson (~1,000 K) surface area match the city’s heat output—while mirrored thermal baffles along the ring undersides shield Jhilvāng’s cloud decks from downward infrared glare.

Archivist’s note · Planetary Carrying Covenants and fifty millennia of calm

Holding 513 diverse species steady across fifty thousand years without coercing fertility requires balance on both sides of replacement. No family is ever barred from having children, yet once a garden world reaches its chosen ceiling, surplus youth from pronatalist or fast-breeding clades (such as the Tekhvan) claim their full Hearth rights in orbitals, kōṣ, City-Worlds, or newly metered Tabūḷ VIII frontier stars—turning demographic vigour into voluntary settlement rather than crowding.

The Lantern-Worlds (kōṣ)

A kōṣ holds no inner sun. Turning smoothly once every twenty-four hours, its wide equatorial sky-opening gives interior cities a rhythm found nowhere on natural planets: roughly three and a half hours of bright direct noon, fifteen hours of luminous concave-bowl twilight as sunlight sweeps slowly across the curved continents overhead, and five hours of deep true night. Directly beneath the overhanging lip of the opening, where sunlight never falls straight, lie permanent nocturnal oceans and twilight sanctuaries.

Suspended across the outer half of that great oculus on hairline metallic tethers hangs a colossal gilded jāli-web of tilting crystal lenses and mirror-vanes—looking from the ground below like a palace lattice spun across the heavens—softening noon glare and bending warm amber morning and evening light gently down into shadowed interior valleys without breaking the five-hour night. On the outside, its armoured shell radiates trapped cavity sunlight and Fold heat into space in a soft lantern-amber glow.

Archivist’s note · Why a Lantern-World holds its sky without spinning (and why there are no 1-AU Ringworlds)

High-speed centrifugal spin tears apart under 3.5g Vāṣkar terrain, deep Khorvell seas, or naval siege plating. Instead, a kōṣ anchors a static radial 1g bhāṛpaṭ Run-floor against its inner hull, holding charge like a superconducting loop—immense capital charge to lay down, a modest trickle to maintain, backed by thirty-day emergency reserves.

Because outward 1g gravity drops air pressure to near-vacuum within 150 kilometres of the floor, a 150-km inward-turning rim-wall collar holds the entire atmosphere passively even in total power loss, paired with a lightweight cīrapaṭ radiation veil and wartime ward-shells. And because laying and cooling a continuous Run-floor scales steeply with surface area, even a moon-sized kōṣ is a proud Cluster investment—explaining why 1-AU Ringworlds remain architectural folly.

Dress & regalia

Garments echo classical South Asian draped silhouettes—flowing wraps, pleated waist-cloths, high-collared long coats, gauzy veils, nose-rings, armlets and forehead jewels—loomed from silk-fine interference-coloured metallic thread that shifts violet to emerald as the wearer moves, paired with wrist-translators and biometric seal-rings. Tenēren in office wear the same raised to radiant majesty, adding feathered metallic cooling vanes along shoulders and spine and a floating House crown held aloft by the Light Hand. Marine Khorvell drape jewelled light-mantles; crystalline Ṣilkaṭ wear inlaid gold filigree.

5Crossing the dark

Seven galaxies stay one commonwealth through a single firewalled web of crystal speech and three physical roads through space—each slower or rarer than the next, each shaped by the strict manners of the Fold.

The Two-Glass Web (Dvīkān-jāl): multiplexed Xiōvāk

Xiōvāk, threadspeech, uses split thinstone crystals bound to one shared pool of charge: plucking a filament sounds its twin across any distance instantaneously. Every major town exchange and Cluster hub houses tall violet spires packed with harmonic-multiplexed bundles of thousands of hair-thin split filaments. Paired with dense optical archives ferried through Gates on every Tide, these trunks weave a living Galactic Web—physically divided into two hardware-segregated planes (section 10): an open Sentient Fast Lane for living voices and ordinary servers, and a quarantined Lār (bounded-AI) Bus watched by non-AI cybersecurity bastions.

Borne solely by the 1,000 colossal Cēndhar battle stations, the Cēn cuts its own door straight into the Deep (Xuānum)—asking no sister ring on the far shore.

Archivist’s note · Why the eye of the needle waits to detonate

In ordinary four-dimensional space, a kilogram-to-tonne-scale black hole evaporates in fractions of a microsecond. While the prow pinch holds all seven Fold dimensions unfurled, however, the lowered higher-dimensional Planck threshold expands the eye’s Schwarzschild radius and drops its Hawking temperature, stabilising it for the Cut. The instant prow wards release the pinch at emergence and four-dimensional gravity snaps back, its entire mass converts immediately to radiation (E = mc2)—producing the megaton-to-gigaton farewell star.

6Suttīḷis, the Thin Place

Everything uncanny about the Tenēren began on one heavy twilight world hanging high above the Core of Gravōrum—a place where native life uses chemistry merely to endure, and drinks xiōns to wake.

The world & the Lash

Suttīḷis (1.4g gravity) circles close to a warm orange sun whose night never truly turns black. Packed Core-halo stars burn brighter than lanterns overhead, and the galactic core rises across the ridge like a cold second dawn. Every sixty years, on a long eccentric orbit, a captured, star-feeding neutron star named the Lash swings near—siphoning the orange sun’s outer wind into a fierce flare of hard X-rays while drowning the atmosphere in a gale of free xiōns. For a whole season, curtains of purple-and-gold thread-aurora ripple from horizon to horizon, fringing crimson as they die. Underfoot, blue-black veins of ḍhāṇsiḷ (thinstone) lace every gorge and riverbed, glowing soft violet for decades after the storm moves on.

Archivist’s note · On the sixty-year orbit and flare of the Lash

Suttīḷis orbits its K-type primary (~0.7 solar masses) at ~0.4 AU, well inside the binary’s ~1.9-AU gravitational stability zone. The Lash (~1.4 solar masses) travels on an eccentric sixty-year path (periastron ~10 AU, apastron ~29 AU)—a classical signature of dynamical capture inside the dense Core halo billions of years after its original supernova elsewhere.

Rather than a cold isolated remnant, the Lash is a recycled, weakly accreting pulsar whose magnetosphere wakes whenever periastron carries it through the primary’s outer stellar wind and dust belt. Even tens of watts per square metre of hard X-rays and relativistic pair-wind at that range deliver thousands of lethal human radiation doses per day while shaking loose a flood of free xiōns.

Alien evolution & the mythic veil

Life on Suttīḷis grew along an entirely separate mineral-organic chemistry—flaring violently during each sixty-year Lash and sinking into thrifty stillness across the quiet decades—that passes straight through Earth-descended flesh untouched, so humans and native fauna never once competed for food or niche. Its ascent climbed five stark rungs:

  1. Drinking: Early microbes precipitated grains of thinstone inside their shells to hoard Lash charge like winter sugar.
  2. Fold-Ducking: Spending charge, cells leaned fragile genetic polymers partway into Kṣōng while X-rays tore harmlessly through ordinary space—venting so much waste heat that whole forests steamed white beneath the aurora.
  3. Listening: Thinstone grains fused into mineral crests, spiral horns and resonant dishes, letting hunters hear the Fold-shape of prey straight through solid cliff-stone.
  4. Fold-Motility & Strike: Listening organs grew into working organs—Lift carrying forty-tonne gliders aloft, sloped Shoves striking untouched, Pleat-hides curving blows aside, and Drain pulses dropping rival fliers dead out of the sky.
  5. Radiator Architecture: Because every working pays in heat, native giants grew stepped metallic cooling plates, bismuth-bright filaments, broad radiator-sails and superheated thermal exhaust lances.

Stranded human settlers gazing through violet storm-haze at those six-limbed, crystal-crested giants gave them the oldest mythic names their memory still held—dragons, phoenixes, griffins, unicorns, leviathans. Seen near, the kinship is sculptural rather than anatomical: metallic radiator plumes instead of feathers, tuned lattice horns, resonant slit-faces, and bodies partly submerged in unseen dimensions. Yet the settlers’ first awe never faded; it still shines above every courthouse door across seven galaxies.

The twenty wild groves & minds of the beasts

The oldest living things on Suttīḷis are its groves—colossal mineral-rooted trees drawing thinstone into heartwood to pool a basin of violet slack through the valley soil. Every great beast nests inside a grove so its young can tune their sensing crests to the grove’s harmonic chord. Because each grove gathers only a fixed charge-income from each sixty-year Lash, every patron lineage stays bound to nearly the same wild number age after age, and none holds the slightest wish to leave the pools of Suttīḷis.

Across the fifty-nine quiet years between Lashes, Suttīḷis sinks into an eerie, hushed mineral dreamscape where ancient beasts rest half-merged into bedrock crystal veins, exchanging slow sub-acoustic Fold-memories across continents—only exploding into roaring, incandescent aerial choreography when the neutron star returns. Their minds dwell between beast and starfarer, like the old dragons of legend: proud, solitary, carrying centuries of acoustic memory etched into mineral crests, answering Tenēren in haunting harmonic chords. To a thousand-year elder, those fifty-nine still years pass like a single slow exhale; it greets a twelve-year-old pilgrim at the mossy gate remembering the child’s great-grandmother as though she walked down the canyon yesterday morning.

Three millennia of vaults, the Vêṣ, and the Oath

For roughly three thousand years before Year 0, the stranded ark-humans lived inside deep geothermal vaults beneath Suttīḷis—tending ark crops under lamps while raising quiet-season surface outposts between Lashes. Because Earth biology and native mineral-organic life never competed, human scholars spent millennia healing blighted groves, decoding harmonic chords, and earning a slow, lasting trust with the twenty patron lineages.

Then came the Vêṣ—an interstellar industrial power that saw thinstone solely as heavy-lift fuel. They enslaved the human clans in strip-mines and burned hundreds of native groves until only twenty endured. Facing shared extinction in Year 0, human clan leaders, vault-physicians and the surviving ancient beasts met inside those twenty last groves and willingly took the same desperate gamble together.

Because native mineral-organic life shares no DNA with Earth, beast tissue was never crudely spliced into human blood. Nine volunteers in ten died; twenty lines lived, and their children—tuned from infancy inside the groves—broke the Vêṣ across 412 brutal years of liberation war. Fleeing, the Vêṣ boiled the oceans and sowed the orbits with mines; healing the world and clearing the sky took three thousand years more.

Suttīḷis today: untouched sacred ground

When the Tenēren founded Jhilvāng on the Quiet Spur roughly ninety-three thousand years ago, they made a solemn renunciation: making Suttīḷis the imperial capital would draw trillions of citizens and crush its delicate groves beneath stone and traffic. Instead, they vacated their cradle completely. There are no off-world sanctuaries and no living cities on Suttīḷis. Today it is an untouched mystical origin world—a serene wilderness where the monumental, vine-wrapped marble castles, pale bridges and soaring ruined towers of the ancient Tenēren and Hearth-Born stand surrendered back to forest, aurora and the twenty wild groves. In daily life. No ordinary citizen will ever set foot upon Suttīḷis, and none resent it—its inviolate quiet is the mythic heart of the Keeping. People know the twenty beasts through plaza bronze, orbital documentary archives, nursery rhymes, and the gleaming metallic sigil mounted above every courthouse door in their Sectorate.

Five schools of thought & the Count

No House has drawn steel on another since the Vē̂ḷmor, and palace murder has no place in Sabhā Chīcakkōr. Across ninety-eight millennia of shifting civic eras, artistic flowerings and forgotten reform waves, the Houses have debated openly in council. The current alignment—merely the thirty-ninth recorded in House Ākhyānarēn’s annals—gathers around five sincere schools: Constitutionalists (slow, disciplined law), Preservers (guarding the long peace above all), Solarists (a civilization that stops reaching dies), Egalitarians (widening citizen authority across all 513 peoples), and Forgemasters (every limit is an engineering problem waiting to be stated well). Each House holds an ancestral leaning, yet the schools cut freely across all twenty bloodlines: a fiery Ādhvarōn youth may argue beside the Solarists, and a Vriṣān admiral may vote with the Preservers.

Beneath every speech lies the Count. Across 774 million inhabited worlds and moons, even a one-in-a-billion unsolvable sorrow arrives every week—a binary star destabilising, a plague outrunning quarantine, two Clusters begging for the same relief window. Almost all are settled along the chain; roughly a dozen each year climb past every lower magistrate onto the Ṣaudhār’s desk, amounting to some seven hundred impossible choices across one sixty-year watch. That quiet moral weight—not hunger for crowns—is what puts silver in a sovereign’s hair.

8Law: the Twelve Tabūḷs and the long chain

How does a revered, gleaming caste of demigod autocrats sit atop an eighty-five-quadrillion-strong socialist commonwealth with zero poverty and zero violent revolution? Through a written constitutional contract—consciously echoing Rome’s Twelve Tables—held steady by hard arithmetic on both sides.

Earned majesty on a delicate balance

The Tenēren do not live like ascetics. In their Season of Flame they dwell in breathtaking metallic splendour—palaces, floating crowns and radiant halls funded proudly by the public Commonwealth as visible emblems of the realm’s dignity. Yet no Tenēren holds private commercial monopolies, shares or inheritable estates; the palace legally belongs to the Office and lasts only so long as the House keeps the Trust. Their safety rests on a clear, unhidden equilibrium: they wield immense reverence and genuine executive power, yet they number 56,250 against 85.633 quadrillion citizens and 500 trillion armed personnel—with the 400 trillion ground legions of Bhūmis Maṇḍōr answering exclusively to elected Citizen Marshals and Magistrates. If the galaxies ever turned against them together, even demigods would fall before dusk—a truth every Tenēren learns in their bones during the 15-Year Walk.

Nor do those guardrails make the Tenēren ceremonial ornaments. Within lawful bounds, their authority genuinely bites: the Ṣaudhār and Wardens decide the agonising triages of the Count, redeploy strategic fleets, and order Outer Writ Red Line strikes without waiting on a 513-species parliamentary deadlock. A Sectorate Warden may even withhold a bandhum from a directly elected Sector Marshal judged reckless or factional—a unilateral veto standing unless overturned by a supermajority of Sabhā Sarvavāk.

Inside each House, birth confers duty and eligibility—never automatic inheritance. Direct children of a sitting sovereign or Warden compete blindly alongside the hundreds of Flame peers of their House’s eligibility window on the four-field polymath exam, 15-Year Walk review and warmup record. Only three years ago, the Throne passed from Savēṭrā Ādhvarōn to her nephew Aurēn over her own direct child—living witness that merit governs even House Ādhvarōn.

Archivist’s note · On Attention Politics and curbing electoral oligarchy

In a Sector Marshal election spanning ~85 trillion voters and 18,400 Clusters, abolishing private capital does not abolish power over public attention. To keep Guild Federation boards and Philosophical Schools from hardening into closed kingmaking cabals, sortition-drawn Citizen Media Juries—sampled randomly across each Sector—curate live debate queues and audit the non-AI ranking algorithms on the Sentient Fast Lane, ensuring lesser-known candidates with strong public performance ledgers remain visible.

The bandhum & the Dittār. Inscribed in violet thread-ink beneath the collarbone or wrist, a bandhum binds an officer to their exact one-sentence Focus (dhyēn), glows gold on lawful signature, resigns instantly on consent, and triggers its discord-lock—burning crimson and routing an automatic appeal to Kōḷium Chīngān—whenever a superior transmits an order coded under a Tabūḷ-forbidden class. Senior officers through Subsector Consuls (and frontier Cluster Magistrates paired with knight-commanders) also wear a split emergency seal—forged during ancient Gate-severing raids when shattered hubs left besieged Clusters cut off for weeks; only when thread-exchanges die externally past tolerance and two neighbouring Clusters confirm isolation does the scarlet-gold mark of the Dittār ignite. Lit roughly 11,000 times across 98,000 years, hundreds of reluctant commanders have been promptly relieved by Kōḷium Chīngān tribunals when threads returned, while only four warlords ever turned steel against relief fleets—all four executed.

Sabhā Sarvavāk: the standing Pan-Species Council

Because the Twenty Houses are human-derived Tenēren who grasp human-shaped woes most intuitively, the Keeping maintains a permanent standing Pan-Species Council at Jhilvāng: 513 equal lit seats, one Voice per living species regardless of population (Tabūḷ VII), chosen by each people’s own custom. Ringing the outer shadow of the rotunda stand a handful of dark, unlit obsidian plinths—the Silent Voices, holding permanent memorial for rival civilizations broken or lost across Unification, the Apostasy, and the War of the Lamps. Working year-round through rotating committees and gathering in grand Full Conclave every year (or on emergency call by 100 Voices), Sabhā Sarvavāk wields sharp constitutional teeth: it may summon any Warden onto the public record, compel a mandatory Kōḷium Chīngān audit of any Ministry—or convene an independent 513-species Special Tribunal wholly outside House Īkhen’s chain should Īkhen itself face suspicion—introduce Tabūḷ amendments, co-certify Extinction Alerts or Needle charters, and holds sole authority to pronounce a recalled House cleared after penance.

Archivist’s note · On revoking the Trust and the Navigator’s asymmetry

Amending or revoking Tabūḷ I itself requires no Tenēren signature: a two-thirds supermajority in Sabhā Sarvavāk sends the charter straight to a seven-galaxy citizen referendum across the 18.4 million Clusters. Nor could House Vriṣān turn the Void Sphere against the realm, since ordinary citizen crews hold every warship’s physical haptic locks.

Yet constitutional jurists acknowledge a sharper practical limit: charting the currents of Xuānum demands living xiōkor Listening. The galaxies can lawfully dismiss their guardians tomorrow—but if the Tenēren walk away, the 1,000 Cēndhar Needles go blind.

Archivist’s note · On Senate malapportionment inside Sabhā Sarvavāk

Giving every species one equal Voice under Tabūḷ VII guards rare peoples from cultural erasure, yet creates a sharp constitutional tug-of-war inside the Conclave. Populous, fast-reproducing peoples numbering over two quadrillion souls hold the exact same single ballot as a tiny relic species settled across three Clusters. On heated questions—chiefly Tabūḷ IV lifespan rules and the pace of Tabūḷ VIII star-openings—coalitions of dozens of smaller, longer-lived peoples routinely outvote the demographic giants, feeding an ongoing debate over whether Sabhā Sarvavāk should remain a council of equal peoples or add a population-weighted second chamber.

9Work, time and an ordinary day

Energy and raw matter lie everywhere across Chīcakkōr—every star system holds stellar hydrogen and asteroid belts, and local sun-forges plus wind-nets keep workshops humming. What stays genuinely scarce is living sentient attention, bespoke craft, prime coastline, and heat or slack budgets. Even with ubiquitous non-AI computers tending deterministic factory loops and bright Constrained AGI Lārai drafting plans in moments, Tabūḷ IX forbids machines from acting unguided or bearing moral responsibility—and everything above the ASI ceiling (paradigm-breaking science, diplomacy, authentic art, mentorship and command) belongs permanently to living minds. Because local stars and guilds supply staple abundance inside each Cluster, over 95% of the Keeping’s economy operates as decentralised Cluster-level guild autarky, escaping the brittle blindness of central planning without needing machine gods.

The Civic Hearth & natural mortality for all

Under Tabūḷ V (Tīngōr Sāṃjhā), every citizen holds a generous baseline by birthright: housing tailored to their anatomy, fine regional food, lifelong education, regional Gate transit, a personal Hearth Lār, and complete medicine within their species’ natural biological clock. Just as the Vē̂ḷmor bars Tenēren from halting telomere wear, Tabūḷ IV holds the same line for baseline humans (~110–135 healthy years) and every other people—from forty-year Tekhvan to nine-thousand-year Ōrmeṇ collectives. No clique may artificially outlive its peers to compound permanent superiority; yet short-lived peoples like the Tekhvan periodically reopen the Parity-of-Span debate inside Sabhā Sarvavāk, where Tabūḷ IV has so far held by cross-species covenant.

Guilds & extinguishing vēḷtaḷ time-vouchers

The customary working week is roughly twenty voluntary hours inside member-run, non-profit sector guilds—free of corporations, shareholders or advertisements. Hours beyond twenty, and hours given to scarce, intricate or hazardous craft, earn vēḷtaḷ (measured in hours) through Tīngōr Vēḷis:

  • Extinguishing vouchers: Vēḷtaḷ never circulates person-to-person like money. Spend thirty hours on a master-tailored metal-silk coat or bid in a coastline auction, and those credits are burned and retired by the Clearinghouse while the tailor is credited solely for their actual weighted labour hours. Even the most adored opera singer in seven galaxies cannot amass a fortune to buy a moon.
  • Gentle decay & life-leases: Banked vēḷtaḷ fades by ~5% each year so nine-thousand-year Ōrmeṇ cannot hoard centuries of credit over forty-year Tekhvan. Positional luxuries clear through lifespan-scaled demand auctions, and durable assets (star-yachts, cliffside villas) are life-leases returning eventually to the Hearth at death. Financial plutocracy vanishes, though a warm informal economy of prestige and reciprocal favours naturally blooms—famous artists cannot own a fleet of yachts, yet rarely lack friends eager to host them aboard. Along outer frontiers, quiet grey-market barter in rare art, Ghuḍḍān relics or Free Hold chits keeps Tīngōr Vēḷis mildly busy without ever scratching the Hearth.
  • Clearing hard work without compulsion: Routine hazardous tasks use sentient-guided mechanical rigs paired with generous hazard multipliers—a single hour on a blue-giant sun-forge pays five to ten vēḷtaḷ, letting young crews earn a lifetime star-yacht in two bright, breathless years.

  4. The Glass Cage & Pāḷen Āmā̂n Watchdogs: Every Lār rests inside a sealed optical housing (kāncāl). Faith: the Creed of the Woven Star

Every species keeps thousands of native faiths, songs and silences. Above them rests one gentle, non-theistic civic philosophy: the Creed of the Woven Star. 12War and the knightly orders

The Keeping holds 500 trillion personnel under arms (0.58% of its citizens) inside Sēnā Dvīmaṇḍōr, the Twin-Sphere Host: 400 trillion in Bhūmis Maṇḍōr (planetary legions, garrisons, walker corps and knightly houses—answering constitutionally to elected Citizen Sector Marshals and Cluster Magistrates, never a Tenēren House) and 100 trillion aboard 20 billion vessels in Vōmis Maṇḍōr (the Void Sphere). Its fleets were forged across sixty-six millennia of peer wars and multi-week Cluster sieges, never raised for parades: they project unmistakable deterrence across all seven galaxies so lawless pockets, waking Iron Apostasy derelicts, dormant Devourer bioweapon pods from the Lamps War, and nervous free holds know the exact weight of the Four Red Lines. Yet within the Keeping itself, loyalty never rests on fear—it rests on fifty millennia of proven abundance, while Sēnā Dvīmaṇḍōr thrives in peacetime as the realm’s premier adventure guild, seven-galaxy passport, disaster-rescue vanguard and high-vēḷtaḷ rite of passage.

Void warfare: sight, wind, clipping & starving

Nothing hides in void war; every working blazes in threadlight and cherry-red cooling vanes. At contested outer Gate-yards (10–50 AU), massed drain-lances and clashing capital wards whip up an artificial combat shoal that collapses Runs and skims alike, forcing bitter fusion-torch battles before multi-day pushes inside a star’s natural shoal line reach the inner worlds. Admirals fight for the windward side of magnetars and currents to drink charge while fighting. Warded warships die two ways: clipping (shredding radiator wings until their hulls cook) or starving (bleeding tanks dry with hollow-tuned kō̂ngbhāl drain-lances until wards, Lift and drives fail in one crimson ripple). Because warships physically air-gap internal helm, ward and weapon buses from external web transceivers behind live haptic locks, remote cyber-hijacking is impossible—and close-quarters boarding still settles deadlocks.

“Broad defence, precise offense”: ground & station doctrine

Bound by Tabūḷ VIII and the lasting scar of House Vriṣān’s Burned Reach recall, the Keeping holds planet-cracking firepower strictly as a locked dread-deterrent—refusing to flatten cities or scar biospheres when slavers or fanatics refuse surrender. Instead, agile twin-nacelle assault transports pour living divisions straight onto the objective, which is why Bhūmis Maṇḍōr fields 400 trillion disciplined troops.

  • Ranged & Melee Divisions: Ranged divisions pick targets clean with surgical precision lances and drain-carbines while Melee Divisions anchor frontlines and carve pockets inside tight corridors, hives and bunkers. Close quarters remain vital under the Indoor Rule and against personal Pleat-wards, which turn swift bullets and pulsed fire aside—forcing armoured infantry to drive radial drain-edged spears into direct contact to collapse a ward’s charge pool, backed by lēvār-lightened heavy blades that snap into a directional Run-Shove at the instant of impact.
  • The Aṭaḷdhār Support Walkers (Kōḷium Aṭaḷis): Rather than clumsy tank swarms grinding living streets to dust, ground divisions advance beneath 30-to-50-metre humanoid support colossi piloted by 2-to-4-person bridge crews aided by a haptically locked Grade IV Lār. Rising above urban skyline canyons, their feathered dorsal vanes vent heat safely into open sky while projecting kilometre-wide Pleat-dome shields over whole districts, lifting bunker roofs with ease via weavecraft, dispersing toxic shrouds, and doubling in peacetime as the Keeping’s supreme disaster-rescue engines.
Archivist’s note · On Pleat-ward physics and why heavy armour walks instead of hovers

Personal Pleat-wards are velocity- and impulse-stiff: microsecond high-speed rounds and pulsed beams curve sharply around their steep metre-wide curvature (while kilometre-wide Aṭaḷdhār domes bend distant artillery barrages tens of metres off-target). A close-quarters drain-edged spear remains in continuous lattice contact for milliseconds—long enough to couple into and short-circuit the defender’s charge pool. Heavy melee blades keep lēvār Lift active through the entire swing (preventing mid-air momentum braking) and discharge their hilt micro-capacitor into a millisecond Run-Shove right at impact, grounding recoil through stiffened boots.

Aṭaḷdhār walkers carry legs rather than hover-drives for two thermodynamic reasons: full continuous hovering would bake friendly infantry below in waste heat and drop forty metres of armour onto civilians if drained. Running ~80% Lift-relief and ~20% ground weight on armoured feet slashes thermal bloom, spares city pavement, and hoists radiator plumes and shield emitters clear above surrounding towers.

Archivist’s note · On the two Ages of Expansion, Gate-severing sieges, and the price of the Yoke

The demographic curve and the weight of old losses. Population surged only after pacification. During Unification (10,000–30,000 AF) the Keeping held tens to low hundreds of trillions; losing 61 million in the Hollow Lane was a searing national wound, and a single cut-off Cluster siege routinely butchered 10 to 100 billion citizens. By the Iron Apostasy (45,739 AF), Gravōrum held hundreds of trillions to low quadrillions—meaning machine war killed roughly a tenth to a quarter of all living minds in the galaxy. Only after the Concord of Glass and the Bridging of the Lamps did population swell to 85.633 quadrillion and settle beneath Planetary Carrying Covenants.

Gate-severing raids and non-xiōn rivals. Because 150 to 400 light-years of wild Dark separate neighbouring Clusters, shattering a hub’s Lane Gates before modern Cēndhar existed stranded its worlds for two to five weeks (longer under slower early Runs) while raiders burned planet after planet inside the 12.3-ly pocket—the horror that forged Tabūḷ XI’s Dittār law and the 1,100-ship Cluster garrisons. Rival empires fought peer wars without xiōns through energy-hungry brute-force warp drives, vast older federations outnumbering the young Keeping, and—in the War of the Lamps—ancient Pre-Keeping rift corridors excavated from Ghuḍḍān alongside salvaged Apostasy hulks. With short-range Apostasy Needles unable to bridge 200,000–620,000 light-years of thin halo void, the Lamps War unfolded like an intergalactic battleship campaign fought from halo star-stream to magnetar refueling atoll until forward sister-rings secured each beachhead.

The Pacification Dilemma and the wild nursery. Across two Ages of Expansion, the Keeping concluded grimly that armed multipolar anarchy is a permanent slaughterhouse, and that hard pacification followed by full Tabūḷ V/VII equality is the lesser evil. Descendants of integrated rivals inside Sabhā Sarvavāk challenge that legacy to this day, pointing toward the unlit obsidian plinths of the Silent Voices whenever hawks speak of annexation. Meanwhile, across the 99.993% untouched wild Dark, thousands of younger pre-FTL civilizations still grow undisturbed beneath Tabūḷ VIII’s shield—watched from afar by the Silver Hounds and invited to light a new seat in Sabhā Sarvavāk only when they reach the stars on their own.

Archivist’s note · On the Asymptote, the Rim Schism, and deep time

Strangers often wonder how fifty thousand years can pass without transforming every tool beyond recognition. Under Tabūḷ IV (no posthuman minds) and Tabūḷ IX (no ASI), engineering gradually met the physical ceilings of eleven-dimensional physics—the ~5,000c Run bound, the Planck thresholds of the Cut, the thermodynamic cost of area—a settled plateau historians call the Asymptote. Yet politics and art never froze: civic philosophy rose and shifted in uncounted tides, leaving buried strata kilometres beneath Jhilvāng and archaic pre-Concord legal glosses that modern jurists still read with lexicons.

Even the Keeping’s long peaces carry weathered constitutional scars. During the Rim Schism (58,100–58,900 AF), thirty-seven outer-arm Sectors deadlocked with Jhilvāng over lifespan rules for short-lived peoples, locked their Trunk Gates, and governed themselves as an autonomous Rim League for eight centuries. House Ādhvarōn refused to march legions against citizens who had broken no Red Line; patient diplomacy through Sabhā Sarvavāk eventually drew every Sector back into the Hearth without a shot fired—a precedent historians cite as proof that ancestral stewardship endures through restraint rather than force.

15Quiet cracks and the far sky

In 102,187 AF, no doomsday clock ticks above Jhilvāng. The Keeping stands in the clear evening light of thirty-six millennia without an equal in war. Its stories begin close to the ground—inside the human and alien weather of an immense working commonwealth—before opening a single, shivering window onto what waits past the seventh Lamp.

Layer 1 · Grounded cracks & local story engines

Even a genuine utopia generates continual, high-stakes human and alien friction across 774 million worlds:

  • Nervous Free Holds skirting the Four Red Lines: An ambitious outer-arm microstate hides an unshackled machine-learning black-site or covert debt-bondage mines inside a dust cloud—demanding a surgical Shade-Owl infiltration or a Bhūmis Maṇḍōr and Aṭaḷdhār liberation while keeping the biosphere whole.
  • Waking ghosts of the Wild Dark: Because Tabūḷ VIII bars random sweeps of wild stars, a 55,000-year-old Iron Apostasy machine-fort or a dormant Devourer bioweapon hulk adrift from the Lamps War occasionally wanders out of an unmapped red-dwarf pocket into a Cluster lane, putting local fire-brigade squadrons and Dittār protocols under sudden live fire.
  • Good-versus-good clashes in Sabhā Sarvavāk: Thoughtful, non-villainous collisions—a fast-breeding Tekhvan Cluster bumping against its Planetary Carrying Covenant and asking Tabūḷ VIII to open a pristine wild system an ancient Ōrmeṇ forest considers sacred; Kōḷium Chīngān auditing an overzealous Ministry; or Ilkhūr’s courteous challenge to the permanent guardianship of the Trust.
  • Planetary catastrophes & the Count: A binary star’s tidal flare endangering two worlds inside one Cluster, an Aṭaḷdhār corps bracing a sinking spire-city during a quake, or an agonising deadlock climbing onto Aurēn’s desk.
  • Growing into the Crown: Rāṭhven and Xiōṇī stepping out of sunlit childhood into the exacting 15–30 Academy trials, the quiet ache of Banner Cohort friendships, sibling devotion across two Houses, and the coming 15-Year Walk.

Layer 2 · Beyond the Seventh Lamp: First Contact

Far out in the black halo past Dvā̂ṇis turns a violent nebula lit by a roaring magnetar. 16The tongue and the names

Every native term in this codex belongs to one living imperial tongue: Ṣāngvāk, the High Speech. It keeps the stately syllable cadence and case grammar of Latin, infused with the retroflex stops, voiced aspirates, gemination and clipped rhythm of Punjabi, and crowned with the alveolo-palatal consonants and full four-tone morphology of Sinitic languages.

A hundred thousand years did not simply mash three dictionaries together. Like Norman England or Mughal North India, a trilingual ark society sheltered in geothermal vaults for three thousand years naturally split into two distinct registers bound beneath a single sound system.

Two registers, three ancestor strata

  • The Organic Vault Vernaculars & Core Demotic: In the first centuries underground, everyday speech among Indic, Sinitic and Latinate workers rapidly simplified into a clipped Vault Creole—dropping trailing short vowels, geminating medial clusters and donating earthy craft roots (dhrā́v, dhvōr, ḍhāṇsiḷ, kōṣ, vēḷtaḷ, bhāṛpaṭ, tāpmīr). Over the last fifty millennia, universal Hearth Lārai and Dvīkān-jāl media have sustained modern Core Demotic (Yûgum) as a perpetually refreshed colloquial koine pulled continually back toward High Ṣāngvāk while softening voiced aspirates naturally in casual speech.
  • High Ṣāngvāk & the Chancellery Register: Working from intact ark digital corpora of Classical Latin, Sanskrit/Punjabi and Sinitic, vault scholars deliberately shaped a maximally contrastive high register—like Pāṇinian Saṃskṛta (“refined”)—grafting Latin case architecture and Sinitic four-tone contrasts onto the Vault phonetic skeleton. In Year 0, early xiōkor adepts found that exact pitch, vowel length and aspiration tuned Fold-workings, adopting the register and regularising Old Chinese tonal derivation (biéyì) into a systematic four-tone physics paradigm frozen permanently by neuro-acoustic feedback.

Six diachronic laws of the Vault era

  1. Final apocope (Punjabi flow). No native word ends on a trailing short vowel. Words close on a consonant, a long vowel or a diphthong, trimming away Sanskrit’s trailing -a and Latin’s light -us.
  2. Onset-only aspiration. Aspirated stops and affricates open syllables (including before r, v, y, as in dhvōr, dhrā́v, Ādhvarōn, Rāṭhven) and never close words.
  3. Medial gemination after short vowels. Inside a word after a short vowel, ancient clusters assimilated into Punjabi double consonants (-kr- → -kk-, -kt- → -tt-, -kṣ- → -kkh-, -rt- → -ṭṭ-), giving the drumbeat of Chīcakkōr, Dittār, Ghuḍḍān and Rakkhor. After a long vowel the consonant stays single (Īkhen), while word-initial clusters (dhrā́v, Gravōrum, Vriṣān) survive as learned retentions. Chancellery tatsama compounds (Martikhor, Sarvavāk, Caturdhvōr) keep unassimilated medial clusters, while worn Vault Creole words geminate them.
  4. Palatal fusion & retroflex convergence. Latin soft c, Sanskrit ś/c, and Mandarin j/q/x fused into c, ch, j, jh, x—joined by regular palatalisation of s → x before front vowels i, ē (yielding Xētum, Xīmurgal, Xiphīṇ and Xāntidhār). Indic retroflexes, Mandarin sh/zh/ch, and Latin r-coronal clusters converged into the underdot series ṭ, ḍ, ṇ, ṛ, ḷ, ṣ.
  5. Sinitic compression without killing diphthongs. Raw-Pinyin triphthongs and finals streamlined during the Vault era (-iāo → -iō, -uang → -vāng / -ōng, -uan → -vāṇ, -ui → -ū), while open diphthongs stayed vigorous across both registers.
  6. The Law of Euphony. In Chancellery coinages and standard speech, retroflexes and alveolo-palatals never sit adjacent across a syllable seam, and voiced palatals (j, jh) trigger euphonic de-retroflexion of a following ḷ → l (as in Jhilvāng and rājvēl)—keeping multi-syllable names smooth and resonant.

How people are named

A Tenēren bears three birth names in the Roman fashion: a given name, the House of the other parent in the -is genitive, and their own legally tuned House (upon integrating into a spouse’s household, a married Tenēren shifts their birth House into the -is middle slot). A fourth, earned office name is added only upon entering the Season of Flame at sixty. Thus the sovereign’s full style is Aurēn Khārvanis Ādhvarōn Tāpavid—Aurēn, of House Khārvan by one parent, of House Ādhvarōn, earned title “Knower-of-Heat.” His wife is Mīreṭhā Xiōvārenis Ādhvarōn Sūtradhī; their cross-tuned children are Rāṭhven Ādhvarōnis Xiōvāren and Xiōṇī Xiōvārenis Ādhvarōn. Citizens of the 513 peoples keep their own customs.

Why a maximalist tongue survived 100,000 years

Four structural anchors kept Ṣāngvāk from eroding across a hundred millennia—including the fifty-two millennia before modern Lārai existed:

  • Neuro-acoustic freezing (the decisive anchor). From Year 0 onward, the Tenēren xiōkor answered literally to exact pitch contours, vowel durations and aspiration bursts. The vault scholars’ rich sound grid became a bio-acoustic tuning fork: blurring a tone or dropping a puff threw a working off pitch, locking the standard from the very first generation.
  • Nobody’s mother tongue, everybody’s second. Worlds keep their own lively home languages while Ṣāngvāk sits above them as the unchanging register of law, science, navigation and travel.
  • Cross-species harmonic code. Every consonant, puff and tone maps onto an acoustic-photonic harmonic code playable by any anatomy—pressure chords and skin-light for Khorvell, crystal chimes for Ṣilkaṭ, radio song for Ailūr.
  • Universal home glass. Since the Concord of Glass (52,400 AF), every household Lār speaks classical Ṣāngvāk impeccably to every rising generation, reinforcing the already-frozen standard.

In daily life. A harbour mechanic on a Hearth-Born human world speaks her local island patois at supper, Core Demotic along the docks, and understands High Ṣāngvāk the way an earthbound citizen understands courtroom prose. In Core Demotic—the living colloquial koine of human-descended worlds—voiced aspirates shed their breath and pull the neighbouring vowel down into a low tone. So she never says Yūghum. She says Yûgum, warm and low, the way her grandmother did. Scholars smile at the gentle pun: in High Ṣāngvāk the low hollow tone names absence, meaning billions of Hearth-Born citizens affectionately call their seven-galaxy state “the absent Yoke”—the mild empire they scarcely feel pressing on their shoulders.

Archivist’s note · On Demotic tone-shift and Old Earth’s Punjabi

Historical linguists of House Ākhyānarēn note a subtle fork from ancestral Punjabi: whereas Old Punjabi drew a low tone from word-initial voiced aspirates and a high-falling tone from post-tonic medial ones, Core Demotic generalised the low-tone merger across both positions—aided on hearth-worlds by casual second-syllable stress—bringing everyday Yûgum into accidental rhyme with the High Speech hollow tone.
