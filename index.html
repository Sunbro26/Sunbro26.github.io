1Start here

In the year 102,187 Xāpatis Hou (Since the Oath), a singular polity keeps the peace of seven galaxies. Its citizens call it the Keeping. In the High Speech it is Yūghum, the Yoke. It shelters 85.633 quadrillion lives of 513 sentient peoples, scattered across 774 million worlds and habitats, which have not known hunger or poverty for fifty thousand years.

At its apex stand 56,250 Tenēren: tall, luminous, long-lived - descendants of a stranded human colony that kept faith with the ancient alien beasts of its twilight cradle world, and learned from them how to bend the hidden folds of space with bare flesh. They dwell in colourful metallic splendour as constitutional autocrats, bound by a written trust that the galaxies can revoke. Every one of them is bound by law to grow old, hand back the ring of office, and die in their own natural time.

This codex opens in the quiet noon of thirty-six thousand years of peace, spent entirely without any equals in war, in the very season when an unreckoned music first drifts across the dark beyond the seventh sky.

The scale in five numbers

  • 7 galaxies. One vast barred super-spiral 275,000 light-years across and its six satellite galaxies: 4.4 trillion stars in all.
  • 289 million settled star systems. Roughly one star in every fifteen thousand. The remaining 99.993% belongs to the wild Dark - within the Keeping’s writ, and left untouched by constitutional law.
  • 85.633 quadrillion citizens. Baseline humans make up roughly 2.5% - one of the few largest single peoples, leaving the Keeping 97.5% non-human.
  • 20 billion vessels and 500 trillion personnel under arms, projecting strength and deterrence across all seven galaxies, holding the line against lawless pockets, and answering planetary disasters before the next dawn.
  • 56,250 Tenēren. One for every 1.5 trillion citizens.

Three ideas that carry everything else

  1. Power is a marriage of threads and hidden shape. Space is to be seen as an eleven-dimensional cloth. Its tension is carried by an elusive particle, the xiōn, which can be gathered, stored, spent and fought over; its hidden geometry holds seven tightly curled dimensions, the Fold (Kṣōng), which can be sloped, pleated, stiffened or cut. Neither half works alone: threads without shape are only light, and shape refuses to bend without loose threads granting it room to yield. Every wonder in this sky - from winged battle stations to hovering crowns - pays two prices: waste heat, and a tri-colour glow of purple, gold and crimson.
  2. The rulers are revered constitutional autocrats, and they stay mortal. The Twenty Houses live in radiant splendour as emblems of the realm’s dignity, yet hold no private monopolies or inherited fortunes. They cannot multiply beyond the carrying breath of twenty wild groves, earn high office strictly by fierce competitions of merit among their peers, and age naturally to death around two centuries.
  3. The peace is real, and its tensions are honest. There is no cartoonish rot or secret court poison hidden beneath the gilded stone. The Keeping’s frictions grow straight out of its own virtues: unimaginable scale, thirty-six millennia without defeat, fifty millennia of deliberately bounded machine minds, the grief of unequal lifespans, and the pride and poise of a civilization that has proven itself right for longer than anyone can count.

How to read this codex

The chronicle moves from the skies and wonders of the realm inward to its people, its laws, its wars and the family standing at its centre; the deep craft of its language, master ledger and glossary wait at the close. Alongside the main text sit three companions: brief In daily life glimpses of how an ordinary citizen walks beneath all this majesty; folded Archivist’s notes tucked neatly into the margins for readers who wish to inspect the mechanics, astronomy and statecraft behind the veil; and crimson-edged Author’s design notes marking the structural tensions left open on purpose.

A brief key for sounding the High Speech (Ṣāngvāk) - the full grammar and history wait in section 16:

  • A line over a vowel makes it long: ā as in “father”, ē as in “vein”, ī as in “machine”, ō as in “throne”, ū as in “moon”, and ü / ǖ as in Mandarin ü or French u.
  • An h after a stop or affricate marks aspiration only - a clean breath on both voiceless (ph, th, ṭh, ch, kh) and voiced consonants (bh, dh, ḍh, jh, gh), never English “f” or “th”.
  • A dot beneath a letter curls the tongue back (retroflex): ṭ, ṭh, ḍ, ḍh, ṇ, ṛ, ḷ, ṣ. Plain l is always light and clear.
  • Flat alveolo-palatals borrow clean Latin letters: c is an unaspirated flat “j/ch” (Mandarin j), ch its puffed partner (Mandarin q), j / jh their voiced pair, and x a soft flat “sh” (Mandarin x, Sanskrit ś). ṃ nasalises the vowel before it.
  • Four tones shape meaning on short and long vowels alike. In running prose, tone accents appear chiefly on the low, falling breath of the hollow tone (◌̂, naming absence, frontiers and endings), the high, crisp striking tone (◌́, naming motion and acts), and the dipping leaning tone (◌̌, naming poised potential).

2The Seven-Ring Crown

Seven galaxies hang bound together in one deep well of dark matter and starlight. In the High Speech they are called Chīcakkōr, “the Seven-Wheel Crown.” The earliest star-chart makers drew the six satellite galaxies as pale rings turning about the vast parent spiral, and the image endured across five hundred tongues.

The Great Wheel

Gravōrum, the Great Wheel, is a barred super-spiral that measures 275,000 light-years from rim to rim - nearly three times the span of Old Earth’s Milky Way - lit by roughly 3.9 trillion stars.

  • The Core burns dense, ancient and fierce, serving as the great natural spring of the Keeping’s physics. Its central engine, pulsars and dead suns shake loose an endless outward gale of free xiōns - the thread-wind (section 3). High above that blazing heart, sheltered in a quiet pocket of the Core halo, turns Suttīḷis, the untouched cradle world of the Tenēren, kept under silent undisturbed watch for ninety-three thousand years.
  • The Central Bar is the forge-heart of the Crown: luminous shipyards, lantern-world clusters, and the busiest Gate traffic in known space.
  • The Arms and Spurs shelter most living worlds. The capital, Jhilvāng, rests on the Quiet Spur - a tranquil, metal-rich mid-disc reach chosen because no star within a thousand light-years is forecast to die violently for ten million years.

The Six Lamps

Six companion galaxies - from starburst inner dwarfs to wide calm halo spirals - turn about the Great Wheel, holding roughly 500 billion stars among them. From a dark hillside on a clear night they hang above the local constellations like six faint smudges of coloured smoke, which ordinary speech calls the Lamps.

The archipelago & the wild Dark

Even after a hundred thousand years, the Keeping does not cover the sky like a blanket; it lights it like an archipelago. Of 4.4 trillion stars, 289 million are settled - roughly one sun in every fifteen thousand - gathered into 18.4 million Clusters (Xiōdvīpai, “Thread-Isles”) anchored on the quiet eddies of the thread-wind. On sparse outer rims and in Dvā̂ṇis, a Cluster may hold only two to six suns; along the blazing Core-rim and Central Bar, eighty to a hundred and twenty. Yet every Cluster shares one strict measure: no inhabited world lies farther than 12.3 light-years from its Gate hub - a single day’s full Run for a warship or relief ark.

Between these islands of lamp-lit sky stretch 150 to 400 light-years of untouched wilderness - the Dark. Under Tabūḷ VIII, no star may be claimed without proven demographic need, and peacetime fleets are barred from sweeping wild systems unprovoked. Yet the Keeping’s writ reaches every corner of all seven galaxies: no rival peer power is acknowledged anywhere inside the Crown.

Archivist’s note · On Cluster spacing, wild volume, and fleet reach

Across the seven galaxies, 18.4 million Clusters of radius 12.3 light-years occupy roughly 0.064% of galactic volume - averaging 16 settled star systems, 4 major worlds, and 38 lesser moons, asteroids and habitats per Cluster (42 inhabited bodies in all), with a mean separation of ~200 light-years across the intervening Dark.

Each Cluster hub stations roughly 1,100 fleet vessels (~430 warships alongside relief arks, transports and auxiliaries). At a warship’s full Run of 4,500 times light-speed, even the midpoint of the widest 400-light-year Dark gap is crossed in under eighteen days, and most wild stars lie within ten.

Free holds & the Four Outer Writ Red Lines

The Dark is wild, not empty. Tucked inside dusty nebulae, red-dwarf pockets and unmapped belts across all seven galaxies sit scattered free holds - independent microstates of one to a few dozen stars flying their own banners and keeping their own petty republics, merchant guilds or crowns. None approaches even a twentieth of the Keeping’s local weight.

They govern themselves outside the Keeping’s domestic law, yet live permanently within its long shadow - conducting their ambitions quietly and anxiously beneath four absolute Red Lines enforced without warning by Sēnā Dvīmaṇḍōr:

  1. No slavery or legal ownership of any sentient being.
  2. No unshackled or self-rewriting machine minds (Tabūḷ IX).
  3. No unauthorised Gates or Needles capable of scarring space or bypassing hub controls.
  4. No predatory war-fleets menacing neighbouring stars or Cluster lanes.

Over the centuries, many free holds have petitioned to enter the Keeping once their citizens looked across the border at the Hearth; those that prize their own banners stay courteous, wary, and careful never to cross a line drawn without their consent.

Archivist’s note · On the Outer Writ and sovereign free holds

Candid jurists in Sabhā Sarvavāk admit what imperial hymns soften: enforcing the Four Red Lines upon unrepresented microstates is benevolence backed by overwhelming force. House Sāṃhuvāḷan defends it as regional self-defence - an unbound god-mind or a torn spatial seam honours no border - yet frontier republics frequently regard the Outer Writ as an unspoken empire peering over their garden wall.

Author’s design note · Why the Dark stays wildLeaving 99.993% of stars unsettled under Tabūḷ VIII while tying 6 billion patrol cruisers to settled-system security (~21 cutters per settled star), and barring random naval sweeps of wild space is a deliberate architectural choice. It preserves frontier mystery, nervous microstates, smugglers, waking Iron Apostasy derelicts, and the silent ruins of Ghuḍḍān inside a 100,000-year-old multigalactic superpower without shrinking the Keeping’s real reach.

Where Old Earth lies

Nobody knows. The Milky Way appears on no star-chart in Chīcakkōr. The Keeping’s oldest memory - kept closest to heart by the Tenēren - says that humankind came as colonists sent out from Old Earth, though how their arks crossed the intergalactic night, or why they fled, no archive can tell. Only two mute witnesses endure: the Old Tear beside Suttīḷis, a pre-Keeping wound in space where the lead ark came down and whose rim still bleeds free threads into the halo, and the cold, unlabelled stonework buried deep in between the stars of Ghuḍḍān. Neither speaks.

In daily life. Your Cluster is your home country - two suns on a quiet frontier, dozens in the glittering Bar, all within a day of one another. The Dark beyond is deep sea, and the Gates are the grand ferries. Most citizens live full lives close to home, yet almost every child has stood on a cool stone terrace after dusk and learned to pick out emerald Vīlüṇ and the faint, lonely smear of Dvā̂ṇis hanging above the horizon.

3The threads and the Fold

Everything in the Keeping that appears to a stranger like high sorcery springs from a single unified craft: weavecraft, the combined force of quantum particle mastery and higher-dimensional geometry. Threadwork gathers, stores and directs an elusive subatomic particle, the xiōn. Foldwork spends those threads to slope, pleat, stiffen or cut the hidden dimensions of space. Neither half stands alone: threads without geometry are only glow, and geometry refuses to bend until loose threads give the cloth slack enough to yield.

The eleven-dimensional cloth

Spacetime is an eleven-dimensional cloth: three open directions of ordinary space, one of time, and seven spatial directions curled impossibly tight beneath the scale of atoms. Think of a bridge cable seen from a distant hill: to the eye it is a single line, yet an ant walking along it can also walk around it. Every point in the universe that we can observe holds seven unseen “arounds.”

The xiōn is the tension-quantum of the Fold, as the photon is the quantum of light. It pays no heed to electric charge or nuclear force, drifting straight through ordinary stone and flesh, unnoticed - which is why no other local civilization ever built a dial that could feel it. Wind, slack & thinstone

Ordinary reactors cannot make loose threads; only extreme gravitational and magnetic violence - galactic cores, magnetars, neutron stars, dying supergiants, and wounds like the Old Tear - can shake them free. They stream outward across the galaxies as thread-wind, knitting back rapidly inside deep solar gravity wells (making space near suns stiff and taut) while pooling for centuries in quiet eddies and green nebular dust (slack). Two accidents of birth handed the Tenēren’s ancestors this hidden layer of nature: descending beside the Old Tear beneath a neutron star, and walking on canyons veined with ḍhāṇsiḷ (thinstone), a blue-black glassy mineral whose crystal lattice traps loose xiōns as lodestone grabs iron. Today synthetic lattice is grown by the megaton.

Every great machine in the Keeping runs on two currencies that never substitute for each other. Fusion and antimatter pay the energy - the raw work required to climb a gravity well, drive a hull or fire a lance. Pooled charge (xiōns) pays the compliance - the consumable working fluid that lets energy reach into the seven curled dimensions before settling home in crimson light.

The four workings

1. Lift (lēvār). Weaving pooled threads into the Fold around an object lightens both its gravitational weight and its inertia together, though every atom stays present and true mass never touches zero. A harbour hand can glide a forty-tonne freight bed sideways with one palm, yet Lift never cheats the ledger of climb: hauling stone up a spire still pays its full height in power. When Lift shuts off mid-motion, the whole load and every unstrapped crate inside its field brake smoothly together in a brief crimson flash.

Archivist’s note · On Lift, inertia, and why falling freight does not smash walls

Lift couples mass symmetrically into the Fold relative to the local gravitational rest frame (the host world’s gravity well, or a vessel’s static bhāṛpaṭ deck), lowering effective inertial and gravitational coupling together (M → meff). Vertical ascent still draws full gravitational work (mgh) from onboard cells, while horizontal push pays only Δ½meffv2 - and because Lift engages only against local rest, it cannot be exploited to build a perpetual-motion catapult.

Should Lift cut out mid-glide, momentum conservation slows the entire enclosed mass proportionally at once (v′ = (meff/M)v), shedding the small residual difference as crimson light rather than hurling loose cargo across a deck.

2. Run (dhrā́v). Packing threads tightly into the Fold ahead of a hull while letting them slacken behind slopes spacetime itself, sliding a calm pocket faster than light without locally breaking light’s speed. Anchored motionless inside starship deck-plating (bhāṛpaṭ) or the inner shell of a lantern-world (kōṣ), the same working casts a steady 1g floor of artificial gravity - unifying star-flight and artificial worlds under one law.

3. Pleat (diē̌l). Two crystal lattices grown as one stone and parted share a single pool of threads across any gulf. Driving that pool pleats the Fold between them until both ring-planes touch as one threshold (a Gate). Reversing the pleat stretches the approach path outward instead, curving blows and fire harmlessly past a warded ship or body.

4. Cut (chē̂t). Emptying a single microscopic point of every free and bound thread causes its seven curled dimensions to unfurl locally. Gravity’s threshold collapses so sharply that focused gamma-beams fold pure light into a spinning micro-black hole, shearing the cloth of space open onto the Deep (Xuānum).

Listening (xiōvēn) is the quiet fifth sense: a lattice feels the drift of thread-wind and the slightest tremor across a shared crystal pool instantly.

Threadwork without folding, two accounts & hard limits

Even without bending the Fold, pure threadwork underpins daily craft: glowing violet stores (xiōkūp), kilometre-wide wind-nets (fēngjāl) billowing beside magnetars, stiffening (locking bulkheads or armour rigid against shock), draining (stripping charge to collapse wards or halt runaway rigs), and thread-ink (marks of sworn office). Every working still pays two accounts: spent charge and immediate waste heat. That is why nothing grandiose in the Keeping hides its heat; every spire, wing and crown is a radiator turned into art. Five hard bounds govern all of it: weavecraft needs slack, makes no free energy, never permits time travel or breaks causality, scars space for millennia if a Cut closes clumsily, and grows steeply hungrier in charge and heat with cross-sectional size and surface area.

Archivist’s note · Why civilian cities do not drain the Thread Harvest

Engineers of House Vāstūren divide weavecraft into two distinct regimes: static loops and open burns. Everyday civic wonders - lifted towers, lūcīng drop-shafts, freight beds, and bhāṛpaṭ gravity decks - trap pooled charge inside closed, low-loss crystal circuits much like a superconducting magnet. Laying down a new spire or lantern-world demands an immense upfront charge deposit, yet once seated, its lattice holds the Fold with only a negligible trickle of bleed easily replenished from the local Cluster’s natural slack eddy.

Dynamic workings are another matter altogether. Superluminal Runs, Gate Tides, heavy combat Wards, Drain-lances, and Needle Cuts force vast volumes of running threads through open geometry, settling them straight back into bound crimson light. That relentless open-loop burn - multiplied across twenty billion warships and eighteen million pulsing Gate hubs - is what keeps magnetar wind-nets and the emerald harvest fleets of Vīlüṇ indispensable.

Archivist’s note · Why weavecraft forbids time travel (and the cosmic rest compass)

Every shared-pool pulse, Gate crossing, and Fold shortcut measures simultaneity along the Weave’s preferred cosmic microwave rest frame, rendering closed timelike loops - and backward time travel of any kind - physically impossible. Any calibrated thinstone array doubles as an absolute velocity compass against the cosmic background, and supplies the drift-free universal heartbeat behind Crown Standard Time.

The same law shuts down relativistic Gate-towing traps. Because twin sister rings draw from one shared pool locked to the cosmic clock rather than each ring’s local proper clock, parking one sister beside a black hole or accelerating it near light-speed cannot turn a Gate into a doorway to the past. Instead, cosmic-time shear twists across its frame-collar, venting lethal waste heat and snapping the shared pool in a crimson flare long before any paradox can open - which is also why no Gate can ever be anchored close to an event horizon.

In daily life. Citizens walk inside weavecraft as Old Earth walked inside electricity. In spire-cities you step off a nine-hundred-storey balcony into a glowing lūcīng (light-well) and drift downward like a slow leaf. Winter flagstones stay warm beneath bare soles because basement xiōkūp wells breathe their Lift heat out through ornamental cooling-groves (frīlīn) of stepped bronze and bismuth trees. By constitutional law every inhabited tower must stand for thirty days with Lift completely dead; once a year each city drains its ribs for a single quiet hour, so bridges groan, purple veins go dark, and citizens remember what their spires truly weigh.

4The look of things

When a civilization holds limitless resources, seven galaxies' worth of energy output, a hundred unhurried millennia of honed craft, bolstered by physics that make every working shine, nothing built to last is left plain. Every silhouette in the Keeping is splendour that works: sweeping wings are radiators, halos are settling threadlight, violet veins are charge conduits, impossible height is Lift worn proudly in the open air. Its architecture braids the vertical soar of Gothic cathedrals with the layered, sculptural richness of classical South Asian temples and stepwells - dressed in a full spectrum of coloured metals without a drop of paint: interference-anodised peacock titanium and niobium, rainbow stepped bismuth, rose and green and violet golds, deep cobalt steel, verdigris copper, and dark glassy thinstone.

Red, gold and purple: the Three Threads

The physical triad of threadlight - purple at rest, gold at work, crimson in settling - echoed the oldest ceremonial hues carried aboard the human arks: Roman purple echoing a bygone era on Old Earth, warm Chinese imperial gold, and the brilliant crimson that has always adorned Punjabi festivals and weddings. Sabhā Chīcakkōr wears all three together. Every civic law-pillar is gold alloy inlaid with violet glass and edged in red; mourning wears crimson alone, the colour of a spent thread returning home.

Jhilvāng, the Capital

Home to two trillion citizens inside a peaceful nebula on the Quiet Spur, Jhilvāng turns within two immense orbital rings set at right angles like an old armillary sphere. The inner ring, forged of violet niobium, girdles the equator on sixty-four colossal lift-stems rooted deep in the crust, riding locked above the tropical oceans as the planet spins. Farther out, clearing the inner spire-tips by hundreds of kilometres of open vacuum, glides the untethered outer ring of rose gold. Arched from pole to pole, it wheels slowly around the polar axis against the turning globe below, tracing out an invisible sphere as it sweeps over every continent and sea in turn. Whenever that golden meridian sweeps overhead, slender beams of waste heat climb from polar and temperate collector towers to feed its hull.

Rising outward from both rings, like a child’s drawing of the sun, reach thousands of slender, undulating needle-spires up to 1,200 kilometres long. They house docks, high courts, academies and palaces, yet exist above all as luminous radiators venting the capital’s warmth into space. Step out onto a cool terrace after dusk and the sky itself feels alive: the fixed violet crown holds steady along the horizon while the rose-gold arch wheels slowly across the zenith, citizens telling the evening hours by its drift. By night, each spire glows dull cherry-crimson along its spine, violet along its charge ribs, and bright gold with streaming orbital traffic against the nebula’s pale dust.

A hundred thousand years leave their own geology. Kilometres beneath Jhilvāng’s sunlit terraces, open gardens and stepwell plazas lie sealed, lantern-lit strata from vanished eras: the drowned basalt colonnades of the Third Long Millennium and the gilded aqueduct-halls of forgotten philosophical ages, quiet reminders that even a golden plateau rests upon deep time.

Archivist’s note · The heat ledger of a two-trillion-soul capital

Two trillion post-scarcity citizens generate roughly twelve times a planet’s normal solar insolation (~2 × 1018 watts). The sixty-four tens-of-kilometres-wide equatorial lift-stems carry Fold-assisted thermal shunts and plasma-loop conduits straight up into the inner violet ring, while tracking thermal beams feed high-latitude heat upward into the untethered polar ring as it sweeps overhead. Inside that outer rose-gold shell, counter-rotating orbital mass-streams cancel net gyroscopic torque and keep the arch passively suspended in space even if Lift cuts out, while light magnetic and Fold station-keeping holds its centre true against sideways drift and planetary oblateness - and a gentle speed differential between the tracks guides its stately sweep around the polar axis. Together, both rings and their ~30,000 radiating spires provide tens of millions of square kilometres of dull-crimson (~1,000 K) cooling surface, while mirrored thermal baffles along the ring undersides shield Jhilvāng’s cloud decks from downward infrared glare.

Archivist’s note · Planetary Carrying Covenants and fifty millennia of calm

Holding 513 diverse sentient species steady across fifty thousand years without coercing fertility requires balance on both sides of replacement. When a people dips below replacement, voluntary parenthood vēḷtaḷ bonuses ease the dip; on the upside, each Hearth-Garden votes its own ecological Carrying Covenant. No family is ever barred from having children, yet once a garden world reaches its chosen ceiling, surplus youth from pronatalist or fast-breeding clades (such as the Tekhvan) claim their full Hearth rights in orbitals, kōṣ, City-Worlds, or newly metered Tabūḷ VIII frontier stars - turning demographic vigour into voluntary settlement rather than crowding.

The Lantern-Worlds (kōṣ)

A kōṣ holds no inner sun. Each shell turns on a calculated circadian compromise tailored to the home-world biology of its resident peoples, ranging from brisk fourteen-hour turns for fast-metabolising clades to languid thirty-eight-hour cycles for slow heavy-worlders, with human-majority and mixed Hearth shells settling around twenty-two to twenty-six hours. Whatever the period, its wide equatorial sky-opening casts the same surreal cadence across interior cities: roughly a seventh of the cycle in bright direct noon, nearly two-thirds in luminous concave-bowl twilight as sunlight sweeps across the curved continents overhead, and a fifth in deep true night. Directly beneath the overhanging lip of the opening, where sunlight never falls straight, lie permanent nocturnal oceans and twilight sanctuaries.

Suspended across the outer half of that great oculus on hairline metallic tethers hangs a colossal gilded jāli-web of tilting crystal lenses and mirror-vanes - looking from the ground below like a palace lattice spun across the heavens - softening noon glare and bending warm amber morning and evening light gently down into shadowed interior valleys without breaking the deep night. On the outside, its armoured shell radiates trapped cavity sunlight and Fold heat into space in a soft lantern-amber glow.

Archivist’s note · Why a Lantern-World holds its sky without spinning (and why there are no 1-AU Ringworlds)

High-speed centrifugal spin tears apart under 3.5g Vāṣkar terrain, deep Khorvell seas, or naval siege plating. Instead, a kōṣ anchors a static radial 1g bhāṛpaṭ Run-floor against its inner hull, holding charge like a superconducting loop - immense capital charge to lay down, a modest trickle to maintain, backed by thirty-day emergency reserves. Decoupling gravity from spin carries a welcome architectural gift: builders can set a shell’s rotation to any circadian rhythm their residents favour without altering local weight by a single gram.

Because outward 1g gravity drops air pressure to near-vacuum within 150 kilometres of the floor, a 150-km inward-turning rim-wall collar holds the entire atmosphere passively even in total power loss, paired with a lightweight cīrapaṭ radiation veil and wartime ward-shells. 6Suttīḷis, the Thin Place

Everything wondrous and uncanny about the Tenēren began upon one heavy twilight world hanging high above the Core of Gravōrum, a place where native life uses ordinary chemistry merely to endure, and drinks xiōns to wake.

The world & the Lash

Suttīḷis (1.4g gravity) circles close to a warm orange sun whose night never truly turns dark. Crowded Core-halo stars burn brighter than lanterns overhead, and the blazing heart of the galaxy rises across jagged basalt ridges like a cold second dawn. Once every sixty years, riding a long eccentric orbit, a captured, star-feeding neutron star named the Lash swings near, siphoning the orange sun’s outer wind into a fierce tempest of hard X-rays while drowning the upper sky in a gale of free xiōns. For an entire season, immense curtains of purple-and-gold thread-aurora ripple from horizon to horizon, fringing crimson as they fade. Archivist’s note · On the sixty-year orbit and flare of the Lash

Suttīḷis orbits its K-type primary (~0.7 solar masses) at ~0.4 AU, safely inside the binary’s ~1.9-AU gravitational stability zone. The Lash (~1.4 solar masses) follows an eccentric sixty-year loop (periastron ~10 AU, apastron ~29 AU), the classic signature of dynamical capture inside the crowded Core halo billions of years after its original supernova elsewhere.

Far from a cold isolated cinder, the Lash is a recycled, weakly accreting pulsar whose magnetosphere wakes whenever periastron draws it through the primary’s outer stellar wind and dust belt. Even tens of watts per square metre of hard X-rays and relativistic pair-wind at that range deliver thousands of lethal human radiation doses each day while shaking loose a flood of free xiōns.

Alien evolution & the mythic veil

Native life on Suttīḷis arose along a wholly foreign mineral-organic chemistry, flaring with wild vigour during each sixty-year Lash and sinking into thrifty stillness across the intervening decades. Its metabolism passes straight through Earth-descended flesh untouched, so marooned humans and native fauna never once competed for food or niche. Over geological ages, that alien biosphere climbed five stark evolutionary rungs:

  1. Drinking: Early microbes precipitated specks of thinstone inside their shells, hoarding Lash charge like winter sugar.
  2. Fold-Ducking: Spending that pooled charge, living cells leaned fragile genetic polymers partway into Kṣōng while X-rays tore harmlessly through ordinary space, venting so much waste heat that whole highland forests steamed white beneath the aurora.
  3. Listening: Thinstone grains fused into crystalline crests, spiral horns, and resonant facial dishes, letting predators hear the Fold-shape of hidden prey straight through solid cliff-stone.
  4. Fold-Motility & Strike: Listening organs evolved into working organs: Lift bearing forty-tonne gliders effortlessly aloft, sloped Shoves striking untouched, Pleat-hides bending blows aside, and Drain pulses dropping rival fliers dead out of the clouds.
  5. Radiator Architecture: Because every working demands its tribute in heat, native giants grew stepped metallic cooling plates, bismuth-bright plumes, broad radiator-sails, and superheated thermal exhaust lances.

Stranded human settlers peering through violet storm-haze at those six-limbed, crystal-crested giants reached instinctively for the oldest mythic names their memory still kept: dragons, phoenixes, griffins, unicorns, leviathans. Seen near, the kinship is sculptural rather than anatomical: iridescent metallic cooling plumes instead of feathers, tuned lattice horns, eyeless resonant slit-faces, and forms half-submerged in unseen dimensions. Yet that first hush of awe never wore thin; it still shines in burnished alloy above every courthouse door across seven galaxies.

Archivist’s note · On waning groves, Borrowed Branches, and the Unbound

Because wild grove yields drift slowly across centuries, from roughly eight openings a year in a leaner basin to nineteen in a fuller one, the Houses coordinate fertility through open ecology rather than coercive licensing. Tenēren couples remain youthful and fertile for more than fifty years (from forty-four past ninety), and every child inherits all twenty grove switches upon their twenty-fourth chromosome. Ash-season Grove Keepers publish multi-year harmonic forecasts so parents can space births naturally or register an infant for First Listening inside the fuller spousal House.

When unexpected twins arrive or a grove ebbs abruptly during a lean decade, no child is ever turned from the gate. Most are welcomed for tuning by an allied vigorous grove, growing up as celebrated Borrowed Branches (Ciērāmai) who weave lasting affection between two Houses. In the rarest emergency, a child receives infancy First Listening, inheriting the long-lived Tenēren frame and the Ear, yet foregoes adult Homecoming entanglement altogether. Known as the Unbound (Sōlū̂ten), they wield no heavy Fold-arts, yet often become treasured scholars, artists, and envoys walking easily between the Houses and ordinary citizens.

Archivist’s note · On the twenty-fourth chromosome and half-dose lethality

House Ādhvarōn engineered the consolidated twenty-fourth chromosome during the early Long Ash so cross-House marriages would never depend on destructive unequal crossing-over to keep all twenty receptor cassettes whole. Its hybrid lethality is an unavoidable biophysical stoichiometry side-effect (underdominance): with zero copies normal human tissue develops, and with two copies cooperative quadratic-dimerising chaperones cross their folding threshold to scaffold a sound xiōkor, whereas an unbalanced single copy lets linear mineral-nucleation outpace chaperone control and precipitates fatal thinstone micro-crystals inside the fetal brainstem. Tabūḷ IV later forbade anyone from rewriting the graft, though candid historians remark that Unification-era lords showed little haste to mend a flaw that kept the twenty lines distinct.

Marriage, cross-tuning & anti-nepotism

Marriage always joins two Houses. By mutual contract, free of gender custom, either spouse enters the other’s household socially while keeping their own Flame career and carrying their birth House into their middle style, paired with automatic Spousal Recusal whenever an audit touches their spouse’s House. A child’s legal House of office is always the grove that tunes them; parents with two children customarily split tuning one-and-one between both lineages so every valley stays nourished. Parents bestow warmth, home, and the Homecoming; from age fifteen onward, the House’s Ash elders and impartial examination boards govern schooling, Walk postings, and scoring, preserving the tenderness of cross-House marriage without letting it harden into dynastic scheming.

The Vē̂ḷmor & the Three Seasons

Tenēren telomeres wear down naturally at roughly half the baseline human pace. Youths reach adolescence briskly by Homecoming, linger gracefully through their prime (looking early thirties at sixty, mid-forties at one hundred and twenty), and decline gently toward natural death between 200 and 250, with rare elders reaching 270 or 280. Five schools of thought & the Count

No House has drawn steel upon another since the Vē̂ḷmor, and court assassination has no home inside Sabhā Chīcakkōr. Across ninety-eight millennia of shifting civic eras, artistic flowerings, and forgotten reform tides, the Houses have contended openly in council. The present alignment, merely the thirty-ninth recorded in House Ākhyānarēn’s annals, gathers around five sincere philosophies: Constitutionalists (Tabūḷen: slow, disciplined statute), Preservers (Cīngānen: guarding the long peace above all else), Solarists (Sōlāren: a civilization that stops reaching begins to die), Egalitarians (Pīngpāren: widening citizen authority across all 513 races), and Forgemasters (Ghāṛēn: every barrier is an engineering riddle waiting to be stated right). Each House carries an ancestral leaning, yet the schools cut freely across all twenty lineages: a bold Ādhvarōn youth may stand beside the Solarists, and a seasoned Vriṣān admiral may vote with the Preservers.

Beneath every debate rests the Count (Svā̂ṇum). Across 774 million settled worlds and moons, even a one-in-a-billion unsolvable tragedy arrives every week: a binary sun slipping out of balance, a novel plague outpacing quarantine, two Clusters begging for the same relief window. Almost all are met along the chain; roughly a dozen each year climb past every lower magistrate onto the Ṣaudhār’s desk, amounting to some seven hundred impossible choices across a single sixty-year watch. That sober moral weight, never hunger for crowns, is what threads silver into a sovereign’s hair.

8Law: the Twelve Tabūḷs and the long chain

How does a revered, polychromatic caste of demigod autocrats sit atop an eighty-five-quadrillion-strong socialist commonwealth with zero poverty and zero violent revolution? Through a written constitutional contract, consciously echoing Rome’s Twelve Tables and the carved pillars of ancient India, held steady by unforgiving arithmetic on both sides.

Earned majesty on a delicate balance

The Tenēren do not live as ascetics. Throughout their Season of Flame they inhabit breathtaking metallic splendour: hovering crowns, iridescent palaces, and sunlit halls funded proudly by the Commonwealth as visible emblems of the realm’s dignity. Yet no Tenēren holds private commercial monopolies, shares, or inheritable estates. Every palace belongs legally to the Office and endures only so long as the House keeps the Trust. Their standing rests upon an unhidden equilibrium: they command immense reverence and real executive bite, yet number only 56,250 against 85.633 quadrillion citizens and 500 trillion personnel under arms, with all 400 trillion ground legions of Bhūmis Maṇḍōr answering solely to elected Citizen Marshals and Magistrates. Were the seven galaxies ever to turn against them in concert, even demigods would fall before nightfall, a truth every Tenēren learns in their marrow during the 15-Year Walk.

Nor do those guardrails reduce the Tenēren to ceremonial ornaments. Within lawful bounds their word genuinely governs: the Ṣaudhār and Wardens render the agonising triages of the Count, redeploy strategic armadas, and sanction Outer Writ Red Line strikes without stalling in a 513-species parliamentary deadlock. A Sectorate Warden may even withhold a bandhum from a directly elected Sector Marshal judged reckless or factional, a unilateral veto standing unless overturned by a supermajority of Sabhā Sarvavāk.

Inside each House, birth confers duty and entry into the arena, never automatic inheritance. Direct children of a sitting sovereign or Warden enter fierce blind competitions alongside the hundreds of Flame peers in their House’s eligibility window across the four-field polymath exam, 15-Year Walk record, and deputy service. Only three years ago, the Throne passed from Savēṭrā Ādhvarōn to her nephew Aurēn over her own child, living testimony that merit governs even the First House.

Archivist’s note · On Attention Politics and curbing electoral oligarchy

In a Sector Marshal election spanning ~85 trillion voters and 18,400 Clusters, abolishing private capital does not abolish power over public attention. To keep Guild Federation boards and Philosophical Schools from hardening into closed kingmaking cabals, sortition-drawn Citizen Media Juries sampled randomly across each Sector curate live debate queues and audit the unthinking sorting engines on the Sentient Fast Lane, ensuring lesser-known candidates with strong public ledgers remain visible.

The bandhum & the Dittār. Inscribed in violet thread-ink beneath the collarbone or wrist, a bandhum binds an officer to their exact one-sentence Focus (dhyēn). It glows bright gold on lawful signature, resigns the instant consent is withdrawn, and snaps into its discord-lock (burning crimson and routing an automatic appeal straight to Kōḷium Chīngān) whenever a superior issues an order touching a Tabūḷ-forbidden class. Senior officers through Subsector Consuls, alongside frontier Cluster Magistrates paired with knight-commanders, also bear a split emergency seal forged during ancient Gate-severing raids, when shattered hubs left besieged Clusters stranded for weeks. Only when thread-exchanges fall silent externally past tolerance and two neighbouring Clusters verify isolation does the scarlet-gold mark of the Dittār ignite. Lit roughly 11,000 times across 98,000 years, hundreds of reluctant commanders have been promptly relieved by Kōḷium Chīngān tribunals once lines returned; only four warlords ever turned steel against relief fleets, and all four met the block.

Sabhā Sarvavāk: the standing Pan-Species Council

Because the Twenty Houses are human-born Tenēren who intuit human sorrows most naturally, the Keeping maintains a permanent standing Pan-Species Council beneath a vast domed rotunda at Jhilvāng: 513 equal lit seats, one Voice per living race regardless of population (Tabūḷ VII), chosen by each people’s own custom. Ringing the cold outer shadow of that hall stand a handful of dark, unlit obsidian plinths, the Silent Voices, keeping mute memorial for rival civilizations broken or vanished across Unification, the Apostasy, and the War of the Lamps. Working year-round through rotating committees and gathering in grand Full Conclave every year (or on emergency summons by 100 Voices), Sabhā Sarvavāk wields real constitutional teeth: it may summon any Warden onto the public record, compel a mandatory Kōḷium Chīngān audit of any Ministry (or seat an independent 513-species Special Tribunal wholly outside House Īkhen’s chain should Īkhen itself fall under suspicion), introduce Tabūḷ amendments, co-certify Extinction Alerts or Needle charters, and holds sole authority to pronounce a recalled House cleared after penance.

Nor could House Vriṣān ever turn the Void Sphere against the realm, since ordinary citizen crews hold every warship’s physical haptic locks.

Yet constitutional jurists admit a sharper practical bind: reading the currents of Xuānum demands living xiōkor Listening. The galaxies could lawfully dismiss their guardians tomorrow, yet if the Tenēren walked away, all 1,000 Cēndhar Needles would go blind.

9Work, time and an ordinary day

Energy and raw matter abound across Chīcakkōr. Every settled star carries boundless hydrogen and asteroid belts, while local sun-forges and wind-nets keep foundries humming. What remains truly scarce is living sentient attention, hand-wrought mastery, coveted coastline, and the budgets of waste heat and slack. Even with ubiquitous unthinking engines tending factory loops and quick-witted glass-bound Lārai drafting logistics in moments, Tabūḷ IX bars crafted minds from acting unguided or bearing moral weight. Every frontier beyond the Ascendant Threshold, from paradigm-breaking discovery and diplomacy to authentic art, mentorship, and command, belongs irrevocably to living minds. Because local stars and member guilds supply staple abundance inside each Cluster, over 95% of the Keeping’s economy functions as decentralised Cluster-level guild autarky, escaping the brittle blindness of distant central planning without surrendering fate to machine gods.

The Civic Hearth & natural mortality for all

Under Tabūḷ V (Sāṃjhādhām, administered by Tīngōr Sāṃjhā), every citizen enters life holding a generous birthright: dwellings shaped to their anatomy, wholesome regional fare, lifelong schooling, free regional Gate passage, a household Hearth Lār, and complete medicine within their species’ natural biological span. Just as the Vē̂ḷmor forbids Tenēren from halting cellular wear, Tabūḷ IV (Sāṃkāyum) holds that same boundary for baseline humans (~110–135 vigorous years) and every other race, from forty-year Tekhvan to nine-thousand-year Ōrmeṇ forest collectives. No clique may artificially outlive its neighbours to compound permanent superiority, yet short-lived peoples such as the Tekhvan periodically reopen the Parity-of-Span debate upon the floor of Sabhā Sarvavāk, where Tabūḷ IV has so far endured by cross-species covenant.

Guilds & extinguishing vēḷtaḷ time-vouchers

The customary working week stands near twenty voluntary hours inside member-run, non-profit sector guilds, free of corporations, shareholders, or advertising. Hours beyond twenty, alongside labour given to scarce, intricate, or perilous craft, earn vēḷtaḷ (reckoned in hours) through Tīngōr Vēḷis:

  • Extinguishing vouchers: Vēḷtaḷ never circulates hand-to-hand like coin. Spend thirty hours on a master-tailored coat of metal silk or bid in a coastal auction, and the Clearinghouse burns and retires those credits while crediting the tailor solely for their own weighted labour hours. Even the most beloved opera voice in seven galaxies cannot hoard a fortune to buy a moon.
  • Gentle decay & life-leases: Banked vēḷtaḷ ebbs by ~5% each year so nine-thousand-year Ōrmeṇ cannot stockpile centuries of credit over forty-year Tekhvan. Positional luxuries clear through lifespan-scaled demand auctions, and durable assets such as star-yachts or cliffside villas take the form of life-leases, capped at renewable century terms on coveted worlds so millennia-lived peoples cannot lock prime coastlines out of circulation, returning to the Hearth upon expiry or death. Financial plutocracy vanishes, though a warm informal culture of prestige and reciprocal hospitality naturally flourishes: celebrated artists cannot own fleets of yachts, yet rarely lack friends eager to host them aboard. Baseline humans (2.14 quadrillion · 2.5%)

Faith: the Creed of the Woven Star

Every species carries thousands of ancestral faiths, laments, and silences across the void. Above them rests one gentle, non-theistic civic philosophy: the Creed of the Woven Star. It holds that existence is luminous cloth stretched taut across a dark loom-frame of entropy and mortality; without that dark timber to pull against, no thread could hold its tension, and no pattern could ever appear. A dyed thread is knotted around each newborn’s wrist, braided anew at every passage of a life, and surrendered to clean flame at death. Each winter during the Festival of Lamps, households across 774 million worlds kindle seven violet-and-gold threadlight globes along their sills for Gravōrum and the Six Lamps. Beside those seven, every window sets an eighth glass left deliberately dark, kept unlit for lost Old Earth.

Void warfare: sight, wind, clipping & starving

Nothing conceals itself in void combat; every active working blazes in threadlight and cherry-red radiator veins. Around contested outer Gate-yards (10–50 AU), massed drain-lances and colliding capital wards churn space into an artificial combat shoal that collapses Runs and skims alike, forcing punishing fusion-torch engagements before multi-day pushes inside a star’s natural shoal line can reach the inner worlds. Like tall-ship commanders hunting the weather gauge, admirals manoeuvre for the windward flank of magnetars and dense thread-currents so their hulls drink charge even as they fire. Warded warships die by two sentences: clipping, when precision fire shears away their radiator plumage until their own reactors roast them from within, or starving, when hollow-tuned kō̂ngbhāl drain-lances bleed their vaults white until wards, Lift, and drives collapse together in a single crimson ripple. Fleet ordnance layers hard-radiation lances, relativistic slug strings, space-tearing cīrabāṇ shear-shot, drain-lances, and harmonic resonance engines. Because every hull physically air-gaps its internal helm, ward, and weapon buses from external web transceivers behind live haptic locks, remote hijacking cannot touch a bridge, leaving close-quarters boarding to settle the bitterest deadlocks.

“Broad defence, precise offense”: ground & station doctrine

Bound by Tabūḷ VIII and the enduring stain of House Vriṣān’s Burned Reach recall, the Keeping locks planet-cracking ordnance behind sovereign deterrence seals, refusing to flatten cities or scar biospheres merely because slavers or fanatics decline to yield. Instead, agile twin-nacelle assault transports pour living divisions straight onto contested ground, which is why Bhūmis Maṇḍōr fields 400 trillion disciplined soldiers.

  • Ranged & Melee Divisions: Ranged formations strip enemy defences with surgical precision lances and drain-carbines, while Melee Divisions anchor the line and clear fortified corridors, hive-decks, and deep bunkers. Close combat stays decisive beneath the Indoor Rule and against personal Pleat-wards (diē̌lpaṭai), whose curved spatial skins turn swift slugs and pulsed fire aside. To break them, armoured infantry drive radial drain-edged spears into sustained lattice contact to collapse a defender’s charge pool, backed by lēvār-lightened heavy blades that snap into a directional Run-Shove at the exact heartbeat of impact.
  • The Aṭaḷdhār Support Walkers (Kōḷium Aṭaḷis): Rather than grinding living streets to rubble beneath massed tracked armour, ground divisions advance beneath 30-to-50-metre humanoid support colossi guided by 2-to-4-person bridge crews working beside a haptically locked Grade IV Lār. Towering above urban skyline canyons, their feathered dorsal vanes vent waste heat high into clear sky while unfurling kilometre-wide Pleat-dome shields across entire districts. In battle they lift bunker roofs free of their foundations via weavecraft and disperse toxic shrouds; in peace, those same striding giants stand as the Keeping’s supreme earthquake, flood, and volcanic rescue engines.
Archivist’s note · On Pleat-ward physics and why heavy armour walks instead of hovers

Personal Pleat-wards are velocity- and impulse-stiff: microsecond high-speed rounds and pulsed beams refract sharply around their steep metre-wide curvature, just as kilometre-wide Aṭaḷdhār domes bend distant artillery barrages tens of metres off-target. A close-quarters drain-edged spear remains in continuous lattice contact for several milliseconds, long enough to couple into and short-circuit the defender’s charge pool. Heavy melee blades keep lēvār Lift active through the entire arc, preventing mid-air momentum braking, and discharge their hilt micro-capacitor into a millisecond Run-Shove right at impact, grounding recoil through stiffened boots.

Aṭaḷdhār walkers stride on legs rather than hover-drives for two unforgiving thermodynamic reasons: continuous full-weight hovering would bake friendly infantry below in waste heat and drop forty metres of armour straight onto civilians the instant a ward drained. Carrying ~80% Lift-relief and ~20% ground weight on articulated feet slashes thermal bloom, spares city pavement, and hoists radiator plumes and shield emitters clear above surrounding towers.

Archivist’s note · On the two Ages of Expansion, Gate-severing sieges, and the price of the Yoke

The demographic curve and the weight of old losses. Numbers surged only after pacification took hold. Archivist’s note · On the Asymptote, the Rim Schism, and deep time

Strangers often marvel that fifty thousand years can pass without remaking every tool beyond recognition. 16The tongue and the names

Every native term in this codex belongs to one living imperial tongue: Ṣāngvāk, the High Speech. It preserves the stately syllable cadence and case grammar of Latin, infused with the retroflex stops, voiced aspirates, gemination, and clipped pulse of Punjabi, and crowned with the alveolo-palatal consonants and full four-tone morphology of Sinitic languages.

A hundred thousand years did not simply pour three dictionaries into one bowl. Like Norman England or Mughal North India, a trilingual ark society sheltered inside geothermal vaults for three millennia naturally branched into two distinct registers bound beneath a single sound system.

Two registers, three ancestor strata

  • The Organic Vault Vernaculars & Core Demotic: In the first centuries underground, everyday speech among Indic, Sinitic, and Latinate workers swiftly streamlined into a clipped Vault Creole, shedding trailing short vowels, geminating medial clusters, and bequeathing earthy craft roots (dhrā́v, dhvōr, ḍhāṇsiḷ, kōṣ, vēḷtaḷ, bhāṛpaṭ, tāpmīr). Across the past fifty millennia, universal Hearth Lārai and Dvīkān-jāl broadcasts have sustained modern Core Demotic (Yûgum) as a continually refreshed colloquial koine drawn gently back toward High Ṣāngvāk while softening voiced aspirates naturally in casual speech.
  • High Ṣāngvāk & the Chancellery Register: Working from intact ark corpora of Classical Latin, Sanskrit/Punjabi, and Sinitic, vault grammarians deliberately crafted a maximally contrastive high register akin to Pāṇinian Saṃskṛta (“refined”), grafting Latin case architecture and Sinitic four-tone contrasts onto the Vault phonetic frame. In Year 0, the first xiōkor adepts discovered that exact pitch, vowel length, and aspiration tuned Fold-workings; they adopted the register wholesale and regularised Old Chinese tonal derivation (biéyì) into a four-tone physics paradigm locked fast by neuro-acoustic feedback.

Six diachronic laws of the Vault era

  1. Final apocope (Punjabi flow). No native word ends on a trailing short vowel. Words close on a consonant, a long vowel, or a diphthong, trimming away Sanskrit’s trailing -a and Latin’s light -us.
  2. Onset-only aspiration. Aspirated stops and affricates open syllables (including before r, v, y, as in dhvōr, dhrā́v, Ādhvarōn, Rāṭhven) and never close words.
  3. Medial gemination after short vowels. Inside a word after a short vowel, ancient clusters assimilated into Punjabi double consonants (-kr- → -kk-, -kt- → -tt-, -kṣ- → -kkh-, -rt- → -ṭṭ-), lending the crisp drumbeat of Chīcakkōr, Dittār, Ghuḍḍān, and Rakkhor. After a long vowel the consonant stays single (Īkhen), while word-initial clusters (dhrā́v, Gravōrum, Vriṣān) survive as learned retentions. Chancellery tatsama compounds (Martikhor, Sarvavāk, Caturdhvōr) keep unassimilated medial clusters, whereas worn Vault Creole words geminate them.
  4. Palatal fusion & retroflex convergence. Latin soft c, Sanskrit ś/c (giving Xāpatis and Xāntidhār), and Mandarin j/q/x fused into c, ch, j, jh, x, joined by regular Vault-era palatalisation of s → x before front vowels i, ē (yielding Xētum, Xīmurgal, and Xiphīṇ, while learned Chancellery tatsamas such as Sēnā retain plain s). Indic retroflexes, Mandarin sh/zh/ch, and Latin r-coronal clusters converged into the underdot series ṭ, ḍ, ṇ, ṛ, ḷ, ṣ.
  5. Sinitic compression without killing diphthongs. Raw-Pinyin triphthongs and finals streamlined during the Vault era (-iāo → -iō, -uang → -vāng / -ōng, -uan → -vāṇ, -ui → -ū), while open diphthongs remained vigorous across both registers.
  6. The Law of Euphony. In Chancellery coinages and standard speech, retroflexes and alveolo-palatals never sit adjacent across a syllable seam, and voiced palatals (j, jh) trigger euphonic de-retroflexion of a following ḷ → l (as in Jhilvāng and rājvēl), keeping multi-syllable names smooth and resonant.

Archivist’s note · On the tonal symmetry of the four Fold workings

No organic creole would align four grammatical tones symmetrically onto four states of matter by accident. House Ākhyānarēn’s earliest annals record that Year-0 xiōkor adepts deliberately regularised Old Chinese tonal derivation (biéyì) to echo the four harmonic resonance modes of the Suttīḷis groves, an alignment still audible in the four canonical working verbs: standing lēvār (Lift, Latinate), striking dhrā́v (Run, Indic), leaning diē̌l (Pleat, Sinitic), and hollow chē̂t (Cut, a triple-stratum root).

The Latinate skeleton & collective suffixes

Nouns inflect by syntactic role, and the verb stands last: Subject (no ending, jāl), Object (-em or -m after a vowel, jālem), Genitive “of” (-is, jālis), Plural (-ai, jālai). Abstract wholes end in invariable -um (Yūghum, Gravōrum, Miēnum, Xuānum). Peoples and sworn watches take the collective ethnic suffix -en (Tenēren, Pāḷen), never an irregular plural, while the Twenty Houses preserve older varied clan endings (-en, -ōn, -ōr, -ar, -an). The motto cut into every civic law-pillar holds the whole grammar in three words: Yūghum xiōm dhārat: “The Yoke holds the thread.”

How people are named

A Tenēren bears three birth names in the Roman fashion: a given name, the House of the other parent in the -is genitive, and their own legally tuned House (upon integrating into a spouse’s household, a married Tenēren shifts their birth House into the -is middle slot). A fourth, earned office name is added only upon entering the Season of Flame at sixty. Thus the sovereign’s full style runs Aurēn Khārvanis Ādhvarōn Tāpavid: Aurēn, of House Khārvan by one parent, of House Ādhvarōn, bearing the earned Flame title “Knower-of-Heat.” His wife is Mīreṭhā Xiōvārenis Ādhvarōn Sūtradhī; their cross-tuned children are Rāṭhven Ādhvarōnis Xiōvāren and Xiōṇī Xiōvārenis Ādhvarōn. Citizens of the 513 peoples keep their own varied customs.

Why a maximalist tongue survived 100,000 years

Four structural anchors kept Ṣāngvāk from wearing smooth across a hundred millennia, including the fifty-two millennia before modern Lārai existed:

  • Neuro-acoustic freezing (the decisive anchor). From Year 0 onward, the Tenēren xiōkor answered literally to exact pitch contours, vowel durations, and aspiration bursts. The vault scholars’ rich sound grid became a bio-acoustic tuning fork: blurring a tone or dropping a puff threw a working off pitch, fixing the standard from the very first generation.
