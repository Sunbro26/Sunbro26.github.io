1Start here

In the year 102,187 After Founding, one state governs seven galaxies. Its citizens call it the Keeping. In the High Speech it is Yūghum, the Yoke. It holds 85.633 quadrillion people of 513 species across 774 million inhabited worlds and habitats, and it has not known poverty for fifty thousand years.

At its apex stand 56,250 Tenēren: towering, long-lived, inhumanly luminous descendants of a stranded human colony tribe that forged a biological treaty with the alien beasts of its cradle world to break free of interstellar occupiers. They can bend higher-dimensional space with their bodies. They live in revered imperial splendour as constitutional autocrats bound by a written trust that the galaxy can revoke, and every one of them is bound by law to grow old and die in their own natural time.

This codex describes that civilization at the quiet summit of thirty-six thousand years without a peer war—just as an unexpected whisper from beyond its seven skies begins to stir the horizon.

The scale in five numbers

  • 7 galaxies. One giant barred super-spiral 275,000 light-years across and its six satellite galaxies: 4.4 trillion stars in all.
  • 289 million settled star systems. Roughly one star in every fifteen thousand. The remaining 99.993% is wild: inside the Keeping’s writ, and left untouched by constitutional law.
  • 85.633 quadrillion citizens. Baseline humans make up roughly 2.5%—one of the two or three largest single peoples, leaving the Keeping 97.5% non-human.
  • 20 billion vessels and 500 trillion personnel under arms, projecting quiet deterrence across all seven galaxies, enforcing outer red lines against lawless pockets, and answering planetary disasters within the day.
  • 56,250 Tenēren. One for every 1.5 trillion citizens.

Three ideas that carry everything else

  1. Power here is a syncretism of threads and higher-dimensional shape. Space is an eleven-dimensional cloth. Its tension is carried by a pervasive particle, the xiōn, which can be gathered, stored, spent and fought over. Its hidden geometry includes seven tightly curled directions, the Fold (Kṣōng), which can be sloped, pleated, stiffened or cut. Neither half works without the other: threads without shape are only light, and shape cannot bend without threads to grant compliance. Every wonder in the setting—from folding-wing battle stations to floating crowns—spends charge and pays out two signatures: waste heat, and a three-colour glow of purple, gold and crimson.
  2. The rulers are revered constitutional autocrats, and they stay mortal. The Twenty Houses do not live like paupers—they embody the majesty of the realm in jaw-dropping metallic splendour—yet their power rests on a razor-thin structural balance: they cannot grow past the wild carrying capacity of twenty sacred groves, hold no private commercial fortunes, earn high office strictly through blind merit alongside their generation peers, and age naturally to death around two centuries.
  3. The utopia is real, and its tensions are honest. There is no cartoonish rot or petty court backstabbing underneath it. Its frictions grow out of its own virtues—enormous scale, thirty-six millennia without fighting an equal, fifty millennia of deliberately capped machine intelligence, cross-species lifespan disparities, and the quiet hubris of a civilization that has empirically proven itself right for a hundred thousand years.

How to read this codex

Each section gives the technical mechanism in the setting’s own vocabulary, followed by an In daily life vignette showing how an ordinary citizen stands inside it. Where a structural flaw or asymmetry is built into the civilization on purpose to fuel future story dilemmas, a marginal Deliberate tension note flags why it exists. Native terms appear in italics on first use beside a plain-English equivalent, and an expansive five-part glossary closes the codex.

A quick key for reading the revamped High Speech (Ṣāngvāk):

  • A line over a vowel makes it long: ā as in “father”, ē as in “vein”, ī as in “machine”, ō as in “throne”, ū as in “moon”. ü / ǖ is the rounded front vowel of Mandarin ü or French u. Diphthongs (ai, au, ei, ou, ia, io, ua) glide smoothly.
  • An h after a stop or affricate means one thing only: aspiration—a clean burst of air on both voiceless (ph, th, ṭh, ch, kh) and voiced consonants (bh, dh, ḍh, jh, gh), never English “f” or “th”. The digraphs sh and zh, and the letters q and w, do not exist in the system.
  • A dot under a letter marks a curled-tongue retroflex: ṭ, ṭh, ḍ, ḍh, ṇ, ṛ, ḷ, ṣ—unifying Punjabi retroflexes (including curled ḷ and flapped ṛ) and Sinitic retroflex ṣ (“sh” curled back) under one mark. Plain l is always a clear, light alveolar l.
  • Repurposed Latin letters mark flat-tongued alveolo-palatals (Chinese flair): c is an unaspirated flat “j/ch” (Mandarin j), ch is its aspirated partner (Mandarin q), j and jh are their voiced forms, and x is a soft flat “sh” (Mandarin x and Sanskrit ś). ṃ nasalises the vowel before it.
  • Four tones operate on both short and long vowels. In running prose, macrons are always written, while tone marks appear mainly on the low breathy hollow tone (◌̂, marking absence, frontiers and endings) and action-distinctive striking nouns (◌́, sharp fall).

2The tongue and the names

Every native term in this codex belongs to one language: Ṣāngvāk, the High Speech. It keeps the crisp, stately syllable architecture and case grammar of Latin, infused with the retroflex stops, voiced aspirates, gemination and clipped rhythm of Punjabi, and crowned with the alveolo-palatal consonants and full four-tone morphology of Sinitic tongues.

A hundred thousand years of evolution did not merely mash three dictionaries together. Rather, like Norman England or Mughal North India, a trilingual ark society trapped in geothermal vaults for three thousand years naturally split into two distinct registers, both governed by one unified sound system.

Two registers, three ancestor strata

  • The Organic Vault Vernaculars & Core Demotic: In the first centuries underground, everyday speech among Indic, Sinitic and Latinate workers rapidly simplified into a clipped Vault Creole—dropping final short vowels, geminating medial clusters and donating earthy craft roots (dhrā́v, dhvōr, ḍhāṇsiḷ, kōṣ, vēḷtaḷ, bhāṛpaṭ, tāpmīr). Over the last fifty millennia, universal Hearth Lārai and Dvīkān-jāl media have kept modern Core Demotic (Yûgum) alive as a perpetually refreshed colloquial koine pulled continually back toward High Ṣāngvāk while softening voiced aspirates naturally in casual speech.
  • High Ṣāngvāk & the Chancellery Register: Working from intact ark digital corpora of Classical Latin, Sanskrit/Punjabi and Sinitic, vault scholars deliberately engineered a maximally contrastive high register—like Pāṇinian Saṃskṛta (“refined”)—grafting classical Latin case architecture and Sinitic four-tone contrasts onto the Vault phonetic skeleton. In Year 0, early xiōkor adepts discovered that exact pitch, length and aspiration tuned Fold-workings, adopting the register and regularising Old Chinese tonal derivation (biéyì) into a systematic four-tone physics paradigm frozen permanently by neuro-acoustic feedback.

Six diachronic laws of the Vault era

  1. Final apocope (Punjabi flow). No native word ends on a trailing short vowel. Words close cleanly on a consonant, a long vowel or a diphthong, stripping away Sanskrit’s trailing -a and Latin’s light -us.
  2. Onset-only aspiration. Aspirated stops and affricates open syllables (including before r, v, y, as in dhvōr, dhrā́v, Ādhvarōn, Rāṭhven) and never close words.
  3. Medial gemination after short vowels. Inside a word after a short vowel, ancient clusters assimilated into Punjabi double consonants (-kr- → -kk-, -kt- → -tt-, -kṣ- → -kkh-, -rt- → -ṭṭ-), yielding the drumbeat of Chīcakkōr, Dittār, Ghuḍḍān and Rakkhor. After a long vowel the consonant stays single (Īkhen), while word-initial clusters (dhrā́v, Gravōrum, Vriṣān) survive cleanly as learned retentions. Learned Chancellery compounds (tatsama coinages such as Martikhor, Sarvavāk and Caturdhvōr) retain unassimilated medial clusters, while worn Vault Creole words geminate them.
  4. Palatal fusion & retroflex convergence. Latin soft c, Sanskrit ś/c, and Mandarin j/q/x fused into c, ch, j, jh, x—joined by regular palatalisation of s → x before front vowels i, ē (yielding Xētum, Xīmurgal, Xiphīṇ and Xāntidhār). Indic retroflexes, Mandarin sh/zh/ch, and Latin r-coronal clusters merged into the underdot series ṭ, ḍ, ṇ, ṛ, ḷ, ṣ.
  5. Sinitic compression without killing diphthongs. Awkward raw-Pinyin triphthongs and finals streamlined during the Vault era (-iāo → -iō, -uang → -vāng / -ōng, -uan → -vāṇ, -ui → -ū), while warm diphthongs (ai, au, ei, ou, io) stayed vigorous across both registers.
  6. The Law of Euphony. In Chancellery coinages and standard speech, retroflexes and alveolo-palatals never sit adjacent across a syllable seam, and voiced palatals (j, jh) trigger euphonic de-retroflexion of a following ḷ → l (as in Jhilvāng and rājvēl)—keeping multi-syllable names open, smooth and resonant.

The Latinate skeleton & collective suffixes

Nouns inflect by syntactic role, and the verb stands last: Subject (no ending, jāl), Object (-em or -m after a vowel, jālem), Genitive “of” (-is, jālis), Plural (-ai, jālai). Abstract wholes end in invariable -um (Yūghum, Gravōrum, Xuānum). Peoples, Houses and sworn watches take the collective ethnic suffix -en (Tenēren, Dhvōrāḷen, Pāḷen)—never an irregular plural. The motto cut into every law-pillar displays the grammar whole: Yūghum xiōm dhārat — “The Yoke holds the thread.”

How people are named

A Tenēren carries three birth names in the Roman manner: a given name, the House of the other parent in the -is genitive, and their own legally tuned House. A fourth, earned office name is added only upon entering the Season of Flame at sixty. Thus the sovereign’s full style is Aurēn Khārvanis Ādhvarōn Tāpavid—Aurēn, of House Khārvan by one parent, of House Ādhvarōn, earned title “Knower-of-Heat.” His cross-tuned children are Rāṭhven Ādhvarōnis Xiōvāren (tuned to his mother’s birth House) and Xiōṇī Xiōvārenis Ādhvarōn (tuned to her father’s House, her given name honouring her mother’s lineage). Citizens of the 513 peoples keep their own customs.

Why a maximalist tongue survived 100,000 years

Naturally, a 3,000-year underground creole would shed case endings and collapse rich consonant grids. Four structural anchors stopped Ṣāngvāk from eroding—especially across the fifty-two millennia before modern Lārai existed:

  • Neuro-acoustic freezing (the decisive anchor). From Year 0 onward, the Tenēren xiōkor responded literally to exact pitch contours, vowel durations and aspiration bursts. The maximalist phonology of the vault scholars became a bio-acoustic tuning fork: mutating a tone or dropping a puff threw a working off pitch, locking the standard from the very first generation.
  • Nobody’s mother tongue, everybody’s second. Worlds keep their own vibrant home languages while Ṣāngvāk sits above them as the unchanging register of law, science, navigation and travel.
  • Cross-species harmonic code. Every consonant, puff and tone maps cleanly onto an acoustic-photonic harmonic protocol playable by any anatomy—pressure chords and skin-light for Khorvell, crystal chimes for Ṣilkaṭ, radio song for Ailūr.
  • Universal home glass. Since the Concord of Glass (52,400 AF), every household Lār speaks classical Ṣāngvāk impeccably to every new generation, reinforcing the already-frozen standard.

In daily life. A harbour mechanic on a Hearth-Born human world speaks her local island patois at dinner, Core Demotic at the docks, and understands High Ṣāngvāk the way a modern citizen understands courtroom prose. In Core Demotic—the living vernacular of human-descended worlds—voiced aspirates long ago softened into a low tone, just as they did in Old Earth’s Punjabi. So she never says Yūghum. She says Yûgum, low and warm, the way her grandmother did. Scholars smile at the quiet pun: in High Ṣāngvāk the low hollow tone marks absence, meaning billions of Hearth-Born citizens affectionately call their state “the absent Yoke”—the gentle empire they scarcely feel pressing on their shoulders.

3The Seven-Ring Crown

The Keeping’s writ spans a gravitationally bound family of seven galaxies. In the High Speech it is Chīcakkōr, “the Seven-Wheel Crown.” Early star charts drew the six satellite galaxies as luminous rings circling the colossal parent spiral, and the name stuck across five hundred tongues.

The Great Wheel

Gravōrum, the Great Wheel, is a super-massive barred spiral 275,000 light-years across—nearly three times the diameter of Old Earth’s Milky Way—holding roughly 3.9 trillion stars.

  • The Core is dense, ancient and violently radiant, acting as the supreme natural spring of the Keeping’s physics. Its supermassive central engine, pulsars and dead stars shake loose an endless outward gale of free xiōns—the thread-wind (section 4). In a quiet, shielded halo pocket above the Core lies Suttīḷis, the untouched cradle world of the Tenēren, sealed under orbital quarantine for roughly ninety-three thousand years.
  • The Central Bar is the industrial forge-heart: orbital yards, lantern-world clusters, and the densest Gate traffic in the Crown.
  • The Arms and Spurs hold the bulk of settled worlds. The capital, Jhilvāng, sits on the Quiet Spur—a tranquil, metal-rich mid-disc arm chosen because no supernova or gravitational disruption is projected within a thousand light-years for ten million years.

The Six Lamps

Six satellite galaxies—ranging from compact companion spirals and dense metal-rich remnants to the sparse outer dwarf of Dvā̂ṇis—orbit the Great Wheel. Together they hold roughly 500 billion stars (about one-ninth of the Crown’s total). From a clear dark hillside they hang beyond the local stars like six faint smudges of light, which ordinary speech calls the Lamps.

The archipelago & the wild Dark

Settled space comprises 289 million star systems out of 4.4 trillion—roughly one star in fifteen thousand, occupying 0.064% of galactic volume. Those stars are gathered into 18.4 million Clusters, each bounded within a maximum radius of 12.3 light-years around a central Gate hub. Because stellar density swings wildly across seven galaxies, Cluster membership scales naturally with the local sky: sparse outer-arm and Dvā̂ṇis Clusters hold 2 to 6 settled systems, while dense Core-rim and Central Bar Clusters hold 80 to 120 settled systems. Across all 18.4 million Clusters, the mean is roughly 16 settled systems, 4 major worlds and 38 lesser moons, asteroids and habitats (42 inhabited bodies in all), separated from neighbouring Clusters by 150 to 400 light-years of wild Dark.

  • Law (Tabūḷ VIII). No star may be settled without proven demographic need, and unprovoked naval reconnaissance sweeps into the 99.993% wild stars are legally restricted unless an anomaly or distress beacon surfaces.
  • Thread-wind knots. Clusters anchor on natural eddies of pooled slack, where Gates open cheaply, city stores refill quickly, and commerce runs effortlessly.
  • The one-day response cap. Regardless of Cluster richness, no inhabited body sits farther than 12.3 light-years from its Cluster Gate hub—one day’s full Run for a fast warship or relief ark, plus a short sub-light skim into orbit.

Even with 99.993% of stars untouched, the Keeping’s writ covers all seven galaxies completely. Each Cluster hub stations roughly 1,100 fleet vessels (~430 warships plus relief arks, transports and auxiliaries); at 4,500 times light-speed, half the widest 400-light-year Dark gap is crossed in under eighteen days, and most points lie within ten. No rival peer power is acknowledged anywhere inside the seven galaxies.

Free holds & the Four Outer Writ Red Lines

The Dark is not politically sterile. Tucked into isolated nebulae, red-dwarf pockets and wild belts across all seven galaxies are scattered free holds—independent microstates spanning one to a few dozen star systems, flying their own banners and keeping their own petty republics, guilds or monarchies. None approaches even 5% of the Keeping’s regional strength.

They remain formally outside the Keeping’s domestic administration, yet live permanently inside its shadow—carrying out every questionable scheme quietly and anxiously beneath four absolute Outer Writ Red Lines enforced without warning by Sēnā Dvīmaṇḍōr:

  1. No slavery or legal ownership of sentient beings.
  2. No unshackled or self-improving AI (Tabūḷ IX).
  3. No unauthorized Gates or Needles capable of scarring space or bypassing hub controls.
  4. No predatory or expansionist war-fleets menacing neighbouring systems or Cluster lanes.

Many free holds eventually petition to join the Keeping once their citizens witness the abundance and dignity across the border; those that prefer independence stay polite, discreet, and careful never to poke the sleeping colossus.

Deliberate tension · Why the Dark stays wildLeaving 99.993% of stars unsettled by Tabūḷ VIII—while keeping 6 billion patrol cruisers tied down to local system security (~21 cutters per settled system) and legally barring random sweeps of wild space—is a conscious architectural choice. It preserves frontier mystery, smuggling pockets, nervous microstates, waking Iron Apostasy derelicts, and enigmatic pre-Keeping ruins in Ghuḍḍān inside a 100,000-year-old multigalactic superpower without shrinking the Keeping’s real reach.

Where Old Earth lies

Nobody knows. The Milky Way appears on no chart in Chīcakkōr. The Keeping’s oldest tradition—cherished especially by the Tenēren—holds that humanity arrived as colonists sent out from Old Earth. How the arks crossed the intergalactic gulf, and why they were launched, remains completely unsolved. One clue endures: the lead ark came down beside Suttīḷis at the Old Tear, an ancient, non-Keeping wound in space whose far edge still bleeds free threads into the halo. Whether the Tear carried the arks, snagged them, or merely marks their grave, no scholar can prove—and the pre-Keeping ruins buried in the dense crust of Ghuḍḍān offer only further silence.

In daily life. Your Cluster is your home country—two suns on a lonely frontier, dozens in the glittering Bar, all within a day of each other. The Dark between Clusters is deep ocean, and the Gates are the grand ferries. Most citizens rarely travel far because everything a full life asks for blooms close to home; yet every child has stood on a cool terrace after dusk and learned to pick out emerald Vīḷüṇ and the pale outer smear of Dvā̂ṇis.

4The threads and the Fold

Everything in the Keeping that resembles high-fantasy magic rests on one unified science: weavecraft, a true 50/50 syncretism of quantum particle control and higher-dimensional geometry. Threadwork harvests, stores and directs a pervasive subatomic particle, the xiōn. Foldwork spends those particles to slope, pleat, stiffen or cut the hidden dimensions of space. Neither half stands alone: particles without geometry are only glow, and geometry refuses to bend without loose threads granting space the compliance to deform.

The picture: an eleven-dimensional cloth

Spacetime is an eleven-dimensional manifold: three extended dimensions of ordinary space, one of time, and seven spatial dimensions tightly compactified far below subatomic scales. Think of a suspension cable seen from miles away: it looks like a one-dimensional line, yet an ant walking along it can also walk around the cable. Every point in space carries seven microscopic “arounds.”

The xiōn is the tension-quantum of the Fold, as the photon is the quantum of light. It ignores electromagnetic and nuclear forces entirely, passing straight through ordinary atoms unnoticed—which is why no other local civilization ever built an instrument capable of sensing it. Wind, slack & thinstone

Loose threads are never created by ordinary lasers or reactors; they are shaken free exclusively by extreme gravitational and magnetic violence—galactic cores, magnetars, neutron stars, supernovae, and enigmatic anomalies like the Old Tear. They stream outward as thread-wind, settling faster inside deep gravity wells (which makes space close to suns stiff and taut) and lingering for centuries in calm eddies or green nebular dust (slack). Two historic accidents handed the Tenēren’s ancestors mastery of this hidden layer: landing beside the Old Tear under a neutron-star companion, and standing on bedrock veined with ḍhāṇsiḷ (thinstone), a blue-black glassy mineral whose crystal geometry traps loose xiōns the way lodestone grabs iron. Today grown synthetic lattice is manufactured by the megaton.

Energy vs. compliance (the strict separation). Every major machine in the Keeping runs on two currencies that never substitute for one another. Fusion and antimatter supply macroscopic energy—the joules required to climb gravity wells, accelerate hulls or fire lances. Pooled charge (xiōns) supplies spatial compliance—the consumable working fluid that allows energy to couple into the seven curled dimensions before settling back as crimson light.

The four workings

1. Lift (lēvār). Weaving pooled threads into the Fold around an object reduces both its gravitational weight and its effective inertial mass (M → meff) symmetrically relative to the local Weave rest frame, though every atom remains present and true mass never hits zero. Lift never creates free energy: raising a mass vertically still pays full gravitational climb (mgh) from onboard power, while pushing a lifted 40-tonne freight bed sideways costs only Δ½meffv2 (and Lift can only engage relative to local rest, preventing perpetual-motion catapults). If Lift shuts off mid-motion, momentum conservation slows the bed and all unstrapped cargo cleanly together as v′ = (meff/M)v in a crimson spark, eliminating runaway crashes.

2. Run (dhrā́v). Packing threads into the Fold ahead of a hull while freeing them behind slopes spacetime itself, letting a flat pocket slide faster than light without locally exceeding light-speed. Anchored statically into hull deck-plating (bhāṛpaṭ) or the interior shell of a lantern-world (kōṣ), the exact same working creates a steady, localized 1g artificial gravity gradient—unifying starship decks and artificial worlds under one single law.

3. Pleat (diē̌l). Two lattices grown as one crystal and split share a single thread pool regardless of distance. Driving that pool pleats the Fold between them until both ring-planes touch as one geometric surface (a Gate). Reversing the pleat stretches the approach path instead, curving incoming fire harmlessly past a warded hull or body.

4. Cut (chē̂t). Stripping a microscopic point of both free and bound threads forces its seven curled dimensions to unfurl locally, dropping the Planck gravity threshold so dramatically that focused gamma-ray lasers collapse pure light into a spinning micro-black hole—shearing the Weave open into the Deep (Xuānum).

6Suttīḷis, the Thin Place

Everything uncanny about the Tenēren began on one heavy, twilight world in the halo of Gravōrum’s Core—where native life runs on chemistry to endure, and on xiōns to thrive.

The world & the Lash

Suttīḷis (1.4g gravity) orbits snugly at ~0.4 AU inside the stable Hill sphere of a warm orange sun whose sky never truly turns black: packed Core-halo stars burn brighter than Venus overhead, and the galactic core rises like a cold second dawn. In a wide, eccentric sixty-year binary (periastron ~10 AU, apastron ~29 AU—a dynamically captured companion whose 1.9-AU stable zone leaves Suttīḷis at ~0.4 AU rock-solid for billions of years, long after the neutron star’s ancient supernova elsewhere in the Core halo), an old neutron star named the Lash swings past every sixty years, raking the planet in lethal relativistic wind and hard X-rays while flooding the atmosphere with a violent gale of free xiōns. For a full season, curtains of purple-and-gold thread-aurora ripple horizon to horizon, fringing crimson as they die. Underfoot, blue-black veins of ḍhāṇsiḷ (thinstone) thread every canyon and riverbed, glowing soft violet for decades after each Lash.

Alien evolution & the mythic veil

Native life on Suttīḷis evolved along an entirely separate mineral-organic biochemistry—accelerated violently during each 60-year Lash and slowed to thrifty stillness across the quiet decades—that passes straight through Earth-descended biology untouched, meaning humans and native fauna never once competed for food or niche. Its evolution climbed five stark rungs:

  1. Drinking: Early microbes precipitated grains of thinstone inside their shells to hoard Lash charge like sugar.
  2. Fold-Ducking: Spending charge, cells leaned fragile genetic polymers partway into Kṣōng while Lash radiation passed harmlessly through ordinary space—shedding immense waste heat so entire forests steamed under the aurora.
  3. Listening: Thinstone grains fused into mineral crests, spiral horns and resonant dishes, letting predators hear the Fold-shape of prey right through solid rock.
  4. Fold-Motility & Strike: Listening organs evolved into working organs—Lift enabling forty-tonne fliers, sloped Shoves striking untouched, Pleat-hides curving claws aside, and Drain pulses dropping rival fliers dead from the sky.
  5. Radiator Architecture: Every working makes heat, so native fauna evolved stepped metallic cooling plates, bismuth-like filaments, broad radiator-sails and superheated thermal exhaust lances.

The stranded human settlers peered through violet storm-haze at these six-limbed, gleaming, crystal-crested giants and projected the oldest mythic archetypes they carried—dragons, phoenixes, griffins, unicorns, leviathans. Up close, the resemblance is hauntingly sculptural: metallic radiator plumes instead of true feathers, tuned lattice horns, mineral-shearing mandibles or resonant slit-faces, and bodies partly submerged in higher dimensions. Yet the mythic grandeur is no illusion—the settlers’ awe survives in every House banner across seven galaxies.

The oldest organisms on Suttīḷis are its groves—colossal mineral-rooted trees drawing thinstone into heartwood to pool a basin of violet slack through the soil. Every great beast nests in a grove so its young can tune their sensing crests to the grove’s harmonic chord. Because a grove gains only a fixed charge-income each sixty-year Lash, each species stays locked to nearly the same wild number age after age, and the ancient beasts have zero desire to abandon the pools of Suttīḷis. Across the fifty-nine quiet years between Lashes, Suttīḷis settles into an eerie, hushed mineral dreamscape where ancient beasts rest half-merged into bedrock crystal veins exchanging sub-acoustic Fold-memories across continents—only exploding into roaring, incandescent aerial choreography when the neutron star swings home.

Once the Tenēren founded Jhilvāng on the Quiet Spur roughly ninety-three thousand years ago, they made a solemn choice: making Suttīḷis the capital would draw trillions of settlers and crush its delicate groves under industry. Five schools of thought & the Count

No House has drawn steel on another since the Vē̂ḷmor, and petty dynastic murder has no place in Sabhā Chīcakkōr. Across ninety-eight millennia of shifting civic eras, artistic renaissances and forgotten reform waves, the Houses have debated openly in public; the current philosophical alignment divides across five sincere schools: Constitutionalists (slow, disciplined law), Preservers (guarding the long peace above all), Solarists (a civilization that stops reaching dies), Egalitarians (widening citizen authority across all 513 peoples), and Forgemasters (every limit is an engineering problem waiting to be stated cleanly).

Beneath every debate lies the Count. Almost all are resolved along the chain; roughly a dozen a year climb past every lower office onto the Ṣaudhār’s desk, totalling some seven hundred impossible choices across one sixty-year watch. That quiet moral weight—not hunger for crowns—is what ages a sovereign.

8Law: the Twelve Tabūḷs and the long chain

How does a revered, opulent caste of demigod autocrats sit atop an eighty-five-quadrillion-strong socialist utopia with zero poverty and zero violent revolution? Through a codified constitutional contract—modeled consciously on Rome’s Twelve Tables—enforced by hard arithmetic on both sides.

Earned majesty on a delicate balance

The Tenēren do not live like paupers. In their Season of Flame they dwell in jaw-dropping metallic splendour—palaces, floating crowns, radiant halls funded magnificently by the public Commonwealth as visible emblems of the realm’s dignity. Yet no Tenēren holds private commercial monopolies, corporate shares or inheritable estates; the palace belongs to the Office and endures only so long as the House keeps the Trust. Their security rests on a delicate, transparent equilibrium: they command immense respect and genuine executive authority, yet there are 56,250 of them against 85.633 quadrillion citizens and 500 trillion armed personnel—reinforced structurally because the 400 trillion ground legions of Bhūmis Maṇḍōr answer exclusively to elected Citizen Marshals and Magistrates rather than any Tenēren House.. If the citizens, guilds and fleet ever turned against them unanimously, even demigods would fall overnight—a reality every Tenēren internalises during their 15-Year Walk.

Where the autocracy actually bites. Constitutional guardrails do not turn the Tenēren into ceremonial figureheads. Within lawful bounds, their executive authority is genuinely unilateral: the Ṣaudhār and Wardens decide the impossible triages of the Count, redeploy strategic fleets, and authorize Outer Writ Red Line strikes without waiting on a 513-species parliamentary deadlock. Crucially, a Sectorate Warden may even withhold a bandhum from a directly elected Sector Marshal judged dangerously reckless or factional—a unilateral veto that stands unless overridden by a supermajority of Sabhā Sarvavāk.

Merit-over-line House succession. Inside each House, birth grants the duty and eligibility to compete—never a rubber-stamped birthright. Direct-line children (such as a sitting sovereign’s son or daughter) are fully eligible, neither barred nor favoured, competing blindly on the four-field polymath examination, 15-Year Walk review and warmup record against all ~250 rising Flame peers inside their House’s 20-year eligibility window (ages 55–75). Only three years ago, the Throne passed cleanly from Savēṭrā Ādhvarōn to her brilliant grand-nephew Aurēn over her own direct child—living proof that merit governs even House Ādhvarōn.

9Work, time and an ordinary day

Energy and raw matter are abundantly cheap across Chīcakkōr—every system holds stellar hydrogen and asteroids, and local sun-forges plus wind-nets keep industry humming. What remains genuinely scarce is living sentient attention, bespoke craft, prime geographic space, and radiator/slack budgets. Even with ubiquitous non-AI computers running deterministic factory loops and brilliant Constrained AGI Lārai drafting plans in microseconds, Tabūḷ IX forbids machines from independently executing physical decisions or bearing legal accountability—and everything above the ASI ceiling (paradigm-breaking science, moral wisdom, cross-species diplomacy, authentic art, mentorship and command) belongs permanently to living minds. Crucially, because fusion, asteroids and local sun-forges supply staple abundance locally, over 95% of the Keeping’s economy operates as decentralised Cluster-level guild autarky—defeating the classic calculation failure of central planning without needing superintelligent machines.

The Civic Hearth & natural mortality for all

Under Tabūḷ V (Tīngōr Sāṃjhā), every citizen receives a sumptuous baseline unconditionally: architecture adapted to their physiology, fine regional cuisine, lifelong schooling, regional Gate transit, a personal Hearth Lār, and complete medicine within their species’ natural biological clock. Just as the Vē̂ḷmor bars Tenēren from halting telomere wear, Tabūḷ IV extends the exact same limit to baseline humans (~110–135 healthy years) and every other species (from 40-year Tekhvan to 9,000-year Ōrmeṇ collectives). The Keeping holds that no clique may artificially outlive their peers’ natural span to compound permanent superiority—yet short-lived peoples like the 40-year Tekhvan still periodically reopen the Parity-of-Span debate inside Sabhā Sarvavāk, where Tabūḷ IV has so far been reaffirmed by cross-species covenant.

Guilds & extinguishing vēḷtaḷ time-vouchers

The customary working week is roughly twenty voluntary hours inside non-profit, member-run sector guilds—free of corporations, shareholders or advertisements. Hours beyond twenty, and hours spent on scarce, intricate or hazardous work, earn vēḷtaḷ (counted in hours) through Tīngōr Vēḷis:

  • Extinguishing (non-circulating) vouchers: Vēḷtaḷ never circulates peer-to-peer as capitalist money. When you spend 30 hours on a bespoke metallic coat or bid in a coastline auction, those credits are burned and retired by the Guild Clearinghouse, while the tailor or artist is credited strictly for their actual labour hours (weighted by craft/difficulty index). Even the most beloved opera diva in seven galaxies cannot accumulate millions of credits to buy up a planet.
  • 5% annual decay & life-leases: Banked vēḷtaḷ gently decays by ~5% per year so 9,000-year-old Ōrmeṇ cannot compound centuries of unspent balance over 40-year-old Tekhvan. Scarce positional luxuries clear via dynamic demand auctions, and durable assets (star-yachts, coastal villas) are life-leases that revert cleanly to the Hearth upon death. Positional life-lease auctions scale bids cleanly against each species’ biological span (so a 40-year Tekhvan is never priced out of a coastline villa by 5% decay). Extinguishing vouchers kill financial plutocracy, though an honest informal economy of social prestige and reciprocal favours naturally blooms—famous artists cannot buy a fleet of yachts, yet rarely lack friends eager to host them aboard. Along outer frontiers, informal grey-market barter in durable valuables—rare art, Ghuḍḍān relics or Free Hold chits—gives Tīngōr Vēḷis persistent low-level headaches without ever threatening the Hearth.

Bound by Tabūḷ VIII and the scar of House Vriṣān’s Burned Reach recall, the Keeping possesses planet-cracking firepower solely as a locked dread-deterrent—refusing to flatten living cities or scar biospheres when fanatics or slavers refuse surrender. A close-quarters fighter in stiffened armour driving a radial drain-edged spear maintains millisecond lattice contact to collapse the defender’s charge pool on contact. Deep in the black halo past Dvā̂ṇis swirls a violent nebula anchored by a roaring magnetar. By pure astrophysical chance, that magnetar sits directly atop a dormant natural fault-line in the Deep (Xuānum)—a quiet, distant sibling of the mysterious Old Tear beside Suttīḷis. The magnetar’s relentless xiōn hurricane keeps the micro-seam agitated and propped roughly 35 to 40 kilometres wide—far too turbulent for civilian ships, yet just wide enough to thread a sleek scout or a single folded, needle-sheathed Cēndhar.

In 102,187 AF, a patrolling Dvā̂ṇis frigate (chief engineer Mārā Kēṇ on watch) catches an unmistakable, artificial harmonic echo washing across the seam from tens of millions of light-years away. Following audited protocol, the Keeping threads a small, nimble exploratory flotilla of Silver Hound and Vriṣān survey cruisers through the turbulent fold—emerging into a vibrant, scrappy, multipolar Far Cluster crowded with rival alien republics, uneasy federations, corporate leagues and regional empires whose technologies sit millennia behind the Keeping’s.

To the nations of the Far Cluster, even a handful of gleaming Keeping survey cruisers is electrifying—and when formal diplomatic contact follows, and a single 120-kilometre Cēndhar colossus threads folded through the seam and unfurls its full seraphic crimson-and-gold wingspan in orbit above their great council worlds, the entire cluster is struck silent by a mix of awe, wonder and sheer existential terror at the seven-galaxy leviathan that has stepped out of the dark.

Layer 3 · Whispers on the far rim (the veiled shadow)

Formal envoys are barely seated before unsettling fragments begin surfacing in diplomatic whispers, trade-lane charts and private Tenēren Listening logs—too sparse to prove, too chilling to dismiss:

  • A dark mirror beyond their border: The multipolar states of the Far Cluster are not alone in their supercluster. Nervous diplomats whisper of an ancient, vastly larger Immortal Hegemony sprawling across dozens of galaxies past their opposite rim—older than the Keeping, ruled by ageless lords who never knew a Vē̂ḷmor, swallowing frontier systems in total, biosphere-crushing conquest.
  • Something waking inside the Deep: Stranger still, several Far Cluster rivals rely on crude, unregulated spatial-rift drives reverse-engineered from Pre-Keeping relics—leaving widening swathes of their star-lanes knotted, hushed and dead. When the envoy Cēndhar’s Tenēren navigators Listen near those torn corridors, their violet eyes go cold: beneath the raw wounds in Kṣōng, they feel a faint, vast, hungry answering pressure moving inside Xuānum itself—as though reckless tearing has woken something native to the Deep, and the rhythmic thrum of Chīcakkōr’s eighteen million pulsing Gates is ringing across the void like a dinner bell.
Deliberate tension · Why the shadow stays veiledNothing in Layer 3 demands an immediate apocalypse. Stories begin with local politics, wonder, first-contact diplomacy and the personal journeys of Aurēn, Mīreṭhā, Rāṭhven, Xiōṇī and ordinary citizens—while these two sparse, intertwined shadows slowly prove that even a hundred-thousand-year utopia cannot stay safely behind its seven golden rings forever.
